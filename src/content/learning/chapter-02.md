## 1. Preparation for Flight and External Walk-Around Inspection

Before every flight, the pilot conducts a structured external pre-flight inspection ("walk-around") beginning at the cabin door, proceeding clockwise around the airframe to inspect control surfaces, tyres, struts, antennas, pitot tube, static vents, oil level, and fuel drains.

![Fig-02-01: Fuel Sampling and Water Contamination Check](Close-up aviation illustration of a fuel strainer cup sample held against the light showing clear blue AVGAS 100LL above a water droplet settlement line and sediment particles)

## 2. Conducting the External Walk-Around Sequence

The external inspection follows a consistent route around the aeroplane so no station is missed even if the pilot is interrupted on the apron.

![Fig-02-02: Complete External Walk-Around Inspection Stations](Top-down diagram of a training aeroplane showing numbered walk-around inspection stations from cabin check, port wing, nose cowling, starboard wing, fuselage, to empennage)

## 3. Cabin Safety, Harness Adjustment and Emergency Equipment

Once aboard, adjust the seat height and fore-and-aft position so full rudder deflection and brake application can be achieved comfortably, secure the four-point harness, and brief the passenger on door latches, fire extinguisher, and emergency egress.

## 4. Emergency Drills and Action in the Event of Fire

Memorise immediate vital actions for engine fire on start-up, electrical fire in the cabin, and emergency cockpit evacuation on the ground.
