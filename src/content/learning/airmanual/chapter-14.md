## Overview

Exercise 14 is the first solo — your first flight with no instructor on board. It is usually a single circuit. The aim is to consolidate what you have learned and show you can fly the circuit safely alone.

## Performance Changes With No Instructor

With one person less on board, the aeroplane is lighter. Expect:

- A **shorter take-off run** and a **better rate and angle of climb**.
- A **lower lift-off and stall speed**.
- A tendency to **float** more in the flare — be ready to hold off a little longer.
- A slightly different trim; re-trim as needed.

Anticipate these changes so the aeroplane does not surprise you.

## Before You Go

Your instructor authorises the solo when you are ready. Before the flight:

- Complete the normal pre-flight preparation and checks.
- Confirm the weather is suitable and within your limits.
- Brief yourself on the circuit, the go-around, and what you will do if anything is not right.

## Flying the Solo Circuit

- Fly the standard circuit and radio calls exactly as practised.
- Keep the approach **stabilised**. If the landing is not right, **go around** — this is always the safe choice.
- Keep a continuous lookout for other traffic.
- Fly accurate, well-spaced legs at the correct height.

## Airmanship

- Do not rush. Fly the aeroplane first; manage the radio and checks around that.
- Keep within your limits and the conditions you briefed.
- When in doubt, go around. A safe second approach is always better than a poor landing.
