## Overview

Exercise 13 brings together the circuit, the approach, and the landing. It covers the normal powered approach and landing, the go-around, departing and joining the circuit, and the flapless, glide, crosswind, short-field, and soft-field variations.

## The Circuit

The circuit is a standard rectangular pattern flown at circuit height around the runway in use. Its legs are: **take-off/upwind, crosswind, downwind, base,** and **final**. Fly accurate legs, keep correct spacing from the runway, and make the standard radio calls. Keep a continuous lookout for other circuit traffic.

On **downwind**, complete the pre-landing checks (for example **BUMFICH** — Brakes, Undercarriage, Mixture, Fuel, Instruments, Carburettor heat, Harness/Hatches), per your POH.

## The Powered Approach and Normal Landing

Fly a **stabilised** approach: a constant approach speed, a steady descent path, in trim, in balance.

- Control the **airspeed with attitude** and the **descent path with power**.
- Use the aiming point: if it rises in the windscreen you will undershoot (add power); if it sinks you will overshoot (reduce power).
- Over the threshold, close the throttle and **round out (flare)** to arrest the descent.
- **Hold off** just above the runway, raising the nose gently as speed decays, so the aeroplane touches down on the **main wheels** at minimum speed, nose-wheel held off.
- After touchdown, lower the nose gently, keep straight with rudder, and brake as required. Protect the nose-wheel from heavy landings.

Common landing faults: ballooning (over-rounding), bouncing, and floating (too fast). If the landing is not right, **go around**.

## The Go-Around

A go-around is a safe rejected landing. Carry it out promptly whenever the approach or landing is not safe:

1. **Full power**, carburettor heat COLD, with right rudder for balance.
2. **Arrest the descent** and establish the climb attitude.
3. **Retract flap in stages** as speed and height allow, holding the climb attitude.
4. Climb away, re-join the circuit, and set up for another approach.

## Departing and Joining the Circuit

- **Departing:** climb straight ahead to a safe height, then turn to leave the circuit on the standard departure path, watching for traffic.
- **Joining:** join by the standard procedure for the aerodrome (for example an overhead join), descend to circuit height on the dead side, and slot into the downwind leg with correct spacing, making the standard calls.

## Variations

- **Flapless approach:** a flatter path, a higher nose and approach speed, poorer forward view, and a longer float and landing roll. Allow for the longer landing distance.
- **Glide approach:** power at idle; manage energy with attitude and path only; a sideslip or flap can steepen the descent if high. Judge the aiming point carefully — you cannot add power.
- **Crosswind:** keep straight with either the **crab** method (point into wind, then de-crab in the flare) or the **wing-down** method (into-wind wing low with opposite rudder). Land on the into-wind main wheel first. Respect the aircraft's maximum demonstrated crosswind.
- **Short field:** approach at the recommended (lower) speed over the threshold, touch down firmly on the aiming point, and brake promptly to stop in the shortest distance.
- **Soft field:** keep weight off the wheels as long as possible — hold the nose-wheel up on landing and keep the aeroplane moving; use flap and a gentle touchdown to protect the gear.

## Wake Turbulence and Hazards

Keep clear of the wingtip vortices of larger aircraft. Land beyond a preceding heavy aircraft's touchdown point, and take off before its rotation point, to stay above its vortices. Watch for wind gradient and gusts on short final.

## Airmanship

- Fly an accurate, well-spaced circuit at the correct height, and make the standard calls.
- Keep the approach stabilised; go around early if it is not.
- Keep straight with rudder in the flare and roll-out, and protect the nose-wheel.
- Maintain a continuous lookout for circuit traffic and wake turbulence.
