## Overview

Exercise 11 is spinning. It has two parts: the incipient (early) spin and the fully developed spin. You will learn what a spin is, how it differs from a spiral dive, and the standard recovery.

## What a Spin Is

A spin is **autorotation**: the aeroplane descends in a steep, rolling, yawing corkscrew with **both wings stalled**, one more deeply than the other. It begins from a stall with yaw present (uncoordinated flight).

- The more-stalled (down-going) wing has more drag and less lift.
- The less-stalled wing has more lift and less drag.
- This imbalance drives the continuous roll and yaw — autorotation.

### Spin vs Spiral Dive — a critical distinction

| | Spin | Spiral dive |
|---|---|---|
| Wings | **Stalled** | **Not stalled** |
| Airspeed | Low and steady | High and increasing |
| Recovery | Standard spin recovery | Reduce power, level the wings, ease out of the dive |

Applying spin-recovery to a spiral dive (or vice versa) is dangerous. Check the airspeed: low and steady = spin; high and rising = spiral dive.

## The Incipient Spin

The incipient spin is the brief transition from an uncoordinated stall into autorotation, before the spin is fully developed. Recognise it early and recover promptly: centralise the controls and apply **opposite rudder** to stop the yaw, then recover from the stall.

## The Four Phases of a Full Spin

1. **Entry** — a stall with yaw starts autorotation.
2. **Incipient** — rotation develops but is not yet stable.
3. **Developed** — a steady rotation rate, airspeed, and descent.
4. **Recovery** — control inputs stop the rotation and the stall.

## Standard Spin Recovery

Follow the POH for your aircraft, but the standard sequence is:

1. **Throttle — CLOSED.**
2. **Ailerons — NEUTRAL** (aileron can worsen the spin).
3. **Rudder — full OPPOSITE** to the direction of rotation.
4. **Control column — move centrally forward** to unstall the wings (pause as the POH specifies).
5. When rotation stops, **centralise the rudder** and ease out of the resulting dive without a secondary stall.

## Limitations and Safety

- Only spin an aircraft that is **approved for intentional spinning** and loaded within limits. Check the category and the POH.
- Complete the **HASELL** check first, with ample height — spins lose significant altitude.
- Decide a minimum recovery height ("floor") and recover by it.

## Airmanship

- Keep well clear of cloud, terrain, and built-up areas, with plenty of height.
- Identify the condition correctly (spin vs spiral dive) before acting.
- Prevent spins in normal flying by keeping the aeroplane in balance near the stall.
