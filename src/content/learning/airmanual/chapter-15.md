## Overview

Exercise 15 is advanced turning. It has three parts: the steep level turn, recovery from unusual attitudes, and the steep descending turn. You will learn to handle high bank angles, high load factors, and the recovery from steep spiral and nose-high situations.

## The Steep Level Turn

A steep turn uses a high bank angle (about 45° or more). The effects of a level turn are magnified:

- **Load factor rises sharply.** At 60° bank, load factor is 2g — the stall speed rises to about 1.4 times the level stall speed.
- **Much more lift is needed**, so pull back firmly to hold altitude, and add power to hold airspeed (the extra lift adds a lot of induced drag).
- The **overbanking tendency** is stronger; hold opposite aileron to keep the bank constant.

Enter by rolling smoothly to the chosen bank, adding back-pressure and power together. Keep the ball centred. To roll out, lead the heading, roll off the bank, and release the back-pressure and power as the wings level.

## Recovery From Unusual Attitudes

An unusual attitude is any attitude not intended in normal flight. Diagnose it from the attitude indicator and airspeed, then recover by category:

- **Nose-high, airspeed decreasing (risk of stall):** apply power, lower the nose toward the horizon, and level the wings. Prevent the stall.
- **Nose-low, airspeed increasing (spiral dive):** close the throttle, level the wings first, then ease out of the dive. Do not pull before the wings are level, or you increase the load factor and may overstress the aeroplane.

The order matters: **power and nose for nose-high; throttle, wings level, then ease out for nose-low.**

## The Steep Descending Turn

A steep descending turn combines a high bank with a descent. Fly it at a **higher airspeed** for a safe margin above the (raised) stall speed. Watch the rate of descent closely, limit the bank, and keep a safe height. Control airspeed with attitude and manage the descent with power/bank. Keep the ball centred.

## Airmanship

- Clear the airspace thoroughly before any steep manoeuvre — the wing and nose hide traffic.
- Respect load-factor and airspeed limits; do not pull hard at low speed or in a dive before levelling the wings.
- Keep plenty of height, and complete HASELL for advanced manoeuvres.
