## Overview

Exercise 19 is an introduction to instrument flying — controlling the aeroplane by reference to the flight instruments alone, without an outside horizon. This is a safety exercise for the visual pilot, to cope if visual reference is lost. It is not a substitute for an instrument rating.

## Why It Matters

Without an outside horizon (for example in cloud or poor visibility), the senses mislead you. The balance organs and "seat of the pants" give false cues, leading to spatial **disorientation**. The instruments give the truth. **Trust the instruments, not your senses.**

## The Flight Instruments

- **Attitude indicator (AI)** — the master instrument. It shows pitch and bank directly.
- **Airspeed indicator (ASI)** — supports pitch information.
- **Altimeter** — shows height; supports pitch.
- **Vertical speed indicator (VSI)** — shows the rate of climb or descent.
- **Direction indicator / heading indicator** — shows heading.
- **Turn coordinator with balance ball** — shows rate of turn and balance.

Together the "six-pack" lets you fly attitude, performance, and balance by reference alone.

## The Scan

Use a **selective radial scan** centred on the attitude indicator:

- Return to the AI between each other instrument.
- Check the instrument relevant to the current task (for example the altimeter to hold height), then come back to the AI.
- Do not fixate on one instrument. Keep a smooth, continuous scan.

## Attitude and Power

The same rule applies: **Power + Attitude = Performance**. Set the attitude on the AI and the power, then confirm the result on the performance instruments (ASI, altimeter, VSI).

## Basic Manoeuvres on Instruments

- **Straight and level:** hold the wings level and the pitch on the AI; confirm with heading, altimeter, and ASI; keep the ball centred.
- **Climb/descent:** set the pitch attitude and power for the climb or descent; confirm the airspeed and rate; level off by leading with attitude, then power.
- **Turns:** use a **Rate 1** turn (3°/sec); set the bank on the AI, confirm with the turn coordinator, keep the ball centred, and hold pitch for level.
- **Changing airspeed:** change power, hold the attitude, let the speed settle, and re-trim.

Make all inputs small and smooth. Avoid large, abrupt changes that upset the scan.

## Recovery From Unusual Attitudes on Instruments

If disoriented:

- **Nose-high, slowing:** add power, lower the nose on the AI, level the wings.
- **Nose-low, speeding up:** reduce power, level the wings on the AI, then ease out of the dive.

Use the AI as the primary reference, cross-checked with the ASI and altimeter.

## Airmanship

- Trust the instruments over your senses; expect disorientation without an outside horizon.
- Keep a smooth, disciplined scan and make small, smooth inputs.
- This is an emergency skill for a visual pilot. Do not fly in cloud or IMC without the proper rating, currency, and a suitable aircraft.
