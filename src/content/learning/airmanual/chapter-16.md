## Overview

Exercise 16 is the forced landing without power — what to do if the engine fails in flight. The aim is to fly the aeroplane to a safe landing in a chosen field, using a disciplined sequence.

## Immediate Priorities

If the engine fails, the first priority is always to **fly the aeroplane**:

1. **Attitude** — lower the nose to the **best glide attitude** and hold the best glide speed.
2. **Field** — pick a landing field into wind, within gliding range.
3. **Checks** — try a restart (the trouble/cause checks), then prepare for the landing.

## Choosing a Field

Assess fields using the "5 S" guide:

- **Size** — long enough to land and stop.
- **Surface** — firm and smooth (avoid soft ground, crops, obstacles).
- **Shape** — a clear run into wind.
- **Slope** — land uphill if possible; avoid a downhill landing.
- **Surroundings** — clear approach, no wires or obstacles; near help if possible.

Land **into wind** where the field allows.

## Causes and the Restart Attempt

Common causes of a stoppage include fuel mismanagement, carburettor icing, and ignition faults. Run the trouble checks: fuel (ON, correct tank, pump on), mixture RICH, carburettor heat HOT, ignition on BOTH, primer locked. If the engine does not restart, commit to the forced landing.

## Flying the Pattern

Plan a glide circuit to arrive with energy to spare:

- Aim to reach a **high key** point over the field, then a **low key** point abeam the aiming point (about 1,000–1,500 ft AGL).
- Fly a base leg and final that let you adjust the path — use flap and sideslip to lose excess height; never stretch the glide if low.
- Complete the **security (pre-impact) checks**: harnesses tight, hatches as briefed, fuel OFF, mixture IDLE CUT-OFF, ignition OFF, master OFF (as the POH directs), to reduce fire risk.

## Making a MAYDAY Call

If height and time allow, make a **MAYDAY** call on the appropriate frequency (or 121.5), squawk 7700, and give your position, intentions, and persons on board.

## Airmanship

- Fly the aeroplane first; never let the restart drills distract you from flying a safe glide.
- Commit to a chosen field early — changing your mind late is a common cause of accidents.
- Maintain the glide speed precisely; do not stretch the glide, and keep a safe margin above the stall.
