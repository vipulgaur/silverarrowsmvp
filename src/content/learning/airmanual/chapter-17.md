## Overview

Exercise 17 is precautionary landings. It has two parts: the precautionary search and landing (on land), and ditching (on water). Unlike a forced landing, here the engine is usually still running, so you can plan carefully before committing.

## The Precautionary Search and Landing

A precautionary landing is a planned landing at an unprepared site when continuing is unwise — for example deteriorating weather, low fuel, approaching darkness, a doubtful engine, or a lost position.

### Decide Early

The key principle is to **decide to land while you still have options** — height, fuel, daylight, and good handling. Do not press on until a forced landing is the only choice.

### The Inspection Procedure

Once you choose a field:

1. Make a **high pass** to assess size, surface, slope, surroundings, and wind.
2. Make a **low pass** along the intended landing path to check the surface closely (soft ground, crops, wires, animals, obstacles).
3. Set up a normal, stabilised approach into wind and land with full flap at minimum speed, ready to go around if the surface looks unsafe.

Carry out the pre-landing and security checks, and make a radio call with your position and intentions.

## Ditching in Water

Ditching is a planned landing on water. It is rare but must be understood.

### Decision and Alerting

Decide early. Make a **MAYDAY** call, squawk 7700, and give your position, intentions, and persons on board. Brief the passengers on the life jackets and the exits, and tell them when to brace.

### Technique

- Fit and tighten life jackets (inflate only **after** leaving the aircraft).
- Approach into wind in light winds; along the swell (not into the face of it) in a large swell.
- Touch down at minimum speed, slightly nose-up, wings level, with a low rate of descent.
- After impact, release harnesses, open the briefed exit, and evacuate; inflate life jackets and deploy the raft once clear.

## Airmanship

- Make the decision to land early, while you still have height, fuel, and daylight.
- Inspect the site thoroughly before committing, and be ready to go around.
- Brief passengers clearly and complete the security checks to reduce injury and fire risk.
