## Overview

Exercise 18 is pilot navigation. It has two parts: cross-country navigation by dead reckoning and map reading, and navigation at lower levels and in reduced visibility. You will learn to plan a route, fly accurate headings and timing, fix your position, and stay safe at low level.

## What Pilot Navigation Is

Pilot navigation (dead reckoning plus map reading) means flying a planned track using heading, airspeed, time, and visual features — not relying solely on radio aids or GPS. You plan on the ground, then fly and check your progress against the map.

## Preparation

Prepare before flight:

- A **flight plan log**: tracks, headings (corrected for wind and variation), distances, groundspeeds, and leg times.
- A **chart** marked with the route, safety altitudes, and prominent features.
- The **navigation satchel**: chart, flight computer (or electronic equivalent), plotter, pen, watch, and the met and NOTAM briefing.
- A fuel plan with reserves.

Apply the wind to the true track to get heading, then apply magnetic variation to get the magnetic heading to fly.

## Flying the Route

- After take-off, settle on the first leg: set heading, note the time, and start the leg timing.
- Use the **1-in-60 rule** to correct track errors: a 1° error gives about 1 NM off track per 60 NM flown. Measure the error and alter heading to regain and maintain track.
- Fix your position by map reading — read from the ground to the map, using prominent, unique features. Confirm the fix with two or more features.
- Revise the groundspeed and ETA at each checkpoint.

## Lost Procedure

If unsure of position: hold the last known heading, note the time, and keep flying while you work it out. Use a prominent line feature (coast, river, railway, motorway) to fix position. Climb (airspace permitting) for a better view and radio range. Ask for help on the radio if needed.

## Low-Level Navigation and Reduced Visibility

Low-level flight is more demanding:

- Maps "unwind" faster and features are harder to identify. Plan larger, more obvious features.
- Keep a continuous lookout for **obstacles** (masts, wires, towers) and **terrain**.
- Watch for **bird and wildlife hazards**, which are greater at low level.
- Avoid congested areas and respect minimum heights.
- In reduced visibility, slow down a little for more thinking time, keep clear of cloud and terrain, and be ready to divert or turn back early.

## Airmanship

- Plan thoroughly on the ground; a good plan makes the flight easy.
- Maintain heading and timing accurately, and fix your position regularly before you become unsure.
- At low level, prioritise obstacle and terrain clearance and a continuous lookout.
- Never press on into deteriorating weather — divert or turn back while you still can.
