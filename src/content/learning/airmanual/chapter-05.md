## Overview

Exercise 5 is about moving the aeroplane safely on the ground. You will learn how to control taxi speed, steer, cross rough ground, hold the controls for the wind, follow the taxi rules, and handle ground emergencies.

## Taxiing Speed

Use power to start moving. Use wheel friction and the brakes to slow and stop.

- An aeroplane resists change (inertia). It takes more power to start rolling than to keep rolling. Once moving, reduce power to hold a steady speed.
- A safe speed on a clear, straight taxiway is a fast walking pace. Use less in a confined area.
- Rough or up-sloping ground needs more power. Turning at low speed may also need more power.
- To slow down, reduce power first. Then use the brakes gently and firmly if needed.

**Do not use power against the brakes.** It wastes energy, overheats the brakes, and wears them. (A few engines need a high idle, where occasional braking is unavoidable.)

### The Brakes

Toe brakes sit on top of each rudder pedal.

- Taxi with your heels on the floor and the balls of your feet on the pedals, so you do not brake by accident.
- To brake, slide your feet up and press the toe brakes with the balls of your feet.
- Brake evenly for a straight stop. Relax the pressure just as the aeroplane halts for a smooth stop.
- **Differential braking** (one toe brake at a time) helps you turn sharply and hold direction in a strong crosswind.
- Test the brakes just after you start to roll, again during a long taxi, and before entering a congested area. Always keep an escape route in mind.

## Ground Surface

- Keep propeller clearance in long grass or over rough ground. Striking grass or ground damages the propeller.
- On loose surfaces, avoid high power — blown stones damage the propeller, the airframe, and anything behind you.
- Cross small ridges or ditches **at an angle**, so the wheels cross one at a time. This reduces stress on the gear and protects the propeller. Avoid large ditches; park and investigate first.

## Wind Effect

Hold the controls so a strong wind cannot lift a wing or the tail.

- **Headwind:** control column neutral or back (elevator neutral/up, tail held down).
- **Tailwind:** control column forward (elevator down, so the wind cannot lift the tail).
- **Crosswind:** the aeroplane tries to weathercock into wind. Steer with the rudder/nosewheel, and use differential braking if needed.
  - Wind from **ahead**: raise the into-wind aileron — move the control column **into** wind.
  - Wind from **behind**: lower the into-wind aileron — move the control column **out of** wind.

A simple summary: when the wind is from **ahead**, hold the column neutral/back and into wind; when it is from **behind**, hold it forward and out of wind. Another aircraft's propwash or jet blast acts like wind — taxi cautiously behind others.

## Taxiing Rules

Five rules keep everyone safe:

1. Whatever any ATC clearance says, it is your duty to avoid collision.
2. Taxiing aircraft give way to aircraft landing or taking off, and to any vehicle towing an aircraft.
3. Approaching head-on, each aircraft turns right.
4. On converging courses, the aircraft with the other on its right gives way.
5. An aircraft being overtaken has right of way; the overtaking aircraft keeps left and clear.

**If in any doubt — STOP.**

## Marshalling

A marshaller may guide you on the ramp, but the pilot is always responsible. Follow a signal only if you judge it safe. In crowds or strong wind, use wingtip assistance or shut down and move by hand or tow-bar.

## Checks While Taxiing

On a straight taxi run, check the rudder for full, free movement and the "directional" instruments:

- **Turning left:** compass and heading decrease; turn indicator shows left; balance ball skids right.
- **Turning right:** compass and heading increase; turn indicator shows right; balance ball skids left.
- The attitude indicator stays level throughout.

## Taxiing Emergencies

### Brake Failure

- Close the throttle.
- Steer away from aircraft and obstacles, toward a higher-friction surface (e.g. grass) if possible.
- If a collision is imminent: mixture IDLE CUT-OFF, magnetos OFF, fuel OFF, master switch OFF.
- When stopped, check the aeroplane and call for assistance. Do not taxi again.

### Steering Failure

- As for brake failure, use differential braking to avoid obstacles. When stopped, call for assistance. Do not taxi again.

## Airmanship

- Keep a good lookout ahead and to each side. Turn slightly left and right if the nose hides the path.
- Follow the taxiing rules. Do not blast debris over aircraft, people, or hangars.
- Operate throttle and brakes smoothly — no sudden stops, starts, or turns.
- If you strike anything, stop, shut down, set the brakes to PARK, and investigate. Do not fly.
