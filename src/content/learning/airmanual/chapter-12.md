## Overview

Exercise 12 is the standard take-off and the climb to the downwind leg of the circuit. You will learn how to take off into wind, judge the ground run, use take-off flap, and fly the first stages of the circuit.

## Take-Off Aerodynamics and Into-Wind Operations

Take off **into wind** whenever possible. A headwind:

- Reduces the groundspeed needed to reach flying speed, so the ground run is shorter.
- Improves the initial climb angle over the ground.

### Determining Surface Wind

Use the windsock, the tower, ATIS, smoke, or surface signs to find the wind direction. Choose the runway most into wind.

### Factors That Lengthen the Ground Run

- **High density altitude** (high elevation, high temperature, low pressure) — less engine power and lift, so a longer run and poorer climb.
- **High weight** — more speed needed to fly, so a longer run.
- **Up-slope, soft, long grass, or wet surface** — more rolling resistance.
- **Tailwind** — avoid; it greatly lengthens the run.

### Take-Off Flap

A small flap setting (about 10°–15°, per the POH) lowers the lift-off speed and shortens the ground run, and can steepen the initial climb for obstacle clearance. Do not use large flap for a normal take-off — the extra drag harms the climb.

## The Take-Off

1. **Line up** into wind and check the runway is clear.
2. **Apply full power** smoothly. Keep straight with rudder (expect left yaw — use right rudder). Check the engine gauges.
3. **Keep straight** along the centreline, looking into the middle distance.
4. **Rotate** at the recommended speed; set the initial climb attitude.
5. **Climb** at the recommended speed (Vx for obstacles, otherwise Vy).
6. **Retract flap** when safely clear of obstacles and above the clean stall speed; hold the attitude and re-trim.

## Climb to the Downwind Leg

Follow the circuit pattern for the aerodrome:

- Climb straight ahead to a safe height before turning.
- Make the **crosswind** turn (usually a climbing turn, limited to about 15°–20° bank), then turn **downwind**.
- Level off at circuit height and set cruise power on the downwind leg.
- Keep a continuous lookout for other circuit traffic, and make the standard radio calls.

## Airmanship

- Always take off into wind where possible, and confirm the wind before lining up.
- Keep straight with rudder during the ground run and initial climb.
- Clear the blind spot in the climb, and fly an accurate circuit pattern at the correct height and spacing.
- Know the aborted take-off action: if a problem occurs before lift-off, close the throttle and stop straight ahead on the remaining runway.
