## Overview

Exercise 9 is turning. It has four parts: the medium level turn, the climbing turn, the descending turn, and turning onto selected headings. You will learn how a bank makes a turn, the effects on lift and stall speed, how to stay in balance, and how to roll out accurately on a chosen heading.

## The Medium Level Turn

A turn is made by banking. Banking tilts the lift force, so part of the lift acts horizontally. This horizontal component is the **centripetal force** that pulls the aeroplane around the turn.

### Holding Altitude and Load Factor

In a level turn, the vertical part of lift must still equal weight. So total lift must increase. This increase is the **load factor (n)**:

- At 30° bank, n ≈ 1.15.
- At 45° bank, n ≈ 1.41.
- At 60° bank, n = 2.0 (you and the aeroplane "weigh" twice as much).

To add the extra lift, raise the nose slightly (more back-pressure) as you roll into the turn. Add a little power to hold airspeed, because the extra lift adds induced drag.

### Stall Speed Rises in a Turn

Higher load factor raises the stall speed. The stall speed increases with the square root of the load factor. At 60° bank (n = 2), the stall speed is about 1.4 times the level stall speed. Steep turns at low speed are dangerous.

### Balance and Adverse Yaw

As you roll in, the down-going aileron adds drag on the rising wing, yawing the nose out of the turn (adverse yaw). Lead with a little rudder in the direction of the turn to stay balanced. Keep the ball centred:

- Ball toward the turn centre → **slipping** → add bank or reduce rudder.
- Ball toward the outside → **skidding** → reduce rudder or add bank.

### Rate and Radius; Overbanking

- A **Rate 1 turn** is 3° per second (a 360° turn takes two minutes). A rule of thumb for the bank: (airspeed in knots ÷ 10) + 7.
- At a steady bank, the outer wing travels faster and makes more lift, giving an **overbanking tendency**. Hold a little opposite aileron to keep the bank constant.

## The Climbing Turn

A climb and a turn together reduce performance. The turn uses some of the excess power, so the **rate of climb falls**. Lower the nose slightly to hold the climb speed. Limit the bank to **15°–20°** so the climb does not suffer too much.

The lower (inner) wing is at a higher angle of attack in a climbing turn, strengthening the overbanking tendency; hold a little opposite aileron. Keep balancing the slipstream yaw with right rudder.

## The Descending Turn

A descending turn can be a glide (no power), powered, with flap, or a sideslipping turn. Key points:

- In a gliding turn, the increased drag raises the rate of descent; the stall speed rises with load factor, so keep a safe margin and limit the bank.
- Control airspeed with attitude and the descent rate with power (in a powered descent).
- The lower wing is at a higher angle of attack, so in a descending turn the overbanking and underbanking couples roughly offset — lateral stability feels neutral. Make small, smooth aileron corrections.
- Keep the engine warm in a prolonged gliding turn, and use carburettor heat per the POH.

## Turning onto Selected Headings

Use one of three references to roll out accurately:

1. **Direction indicator (DI)** — the main method. Start the roll-out before the target heading by about half the bank angle (lead ≈ bank/2). Reset the DI against the compass in steady, level, unaccelerated flight.
2. **Timed turn** — at Rate 1 (3°/sec), time the turn: seconds = heading change ÷ 3. Useful if the DI fails.
3. **Magnetic compass** — direct-reading, but it has turning errors. Remember the northern-hemisphere rules of thumb: **UNOS** (Undershoot North, Overshoot South) — roll out early turning to north, roll out late turning to south. The compass also mis-reads when accelerating or decelerating on east/west headings.

## Flying the Turn

**Enter:** clear the area in the direction of turn; roll on bank with aileron and balance with rudder; add back-pressure (and a little power in a level turn) to hold height and speed.

**Maintain (L-A-I):** **L** — lookout into and around the turn; **A** — hold the bank (a little opposite aileron for overbanking), ball centred, attitude for speed/height; **I** — cross-check the instruments.

**Roll out:** lead the target heading or reference; roll off the bank with aileron and balance with rudder; release the back-pressure (and reset power/attitude) as the wings come level; re-trim.

## Airmanship

- Always clear the airspace in the direction of the turn before rolling in — the wing and nose can hide traffic.
- Keep the aeroplane in balance throughout.
- Respect the higher stall speed in a turn; keep a safe speed margin, especially in steep or low-level turns.
