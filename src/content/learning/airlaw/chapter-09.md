# Chapter 9: Circling Approach

## 9.1 Visual Manoeuvring (Circling): Purpose and Classification

**Visual Manoeuvring (Circling), abbreviated VM(C),** is the visual portion of an approach used when an aircraft has completed an instrument approach but cannot conveniently land directly from that approach onto the intended runway. The pilot manoeuvres visually to reach a suitable position for landing.

The instrument approach preceding the circling manoeuvre may use either precision or non-precision guidance. However, the approach **with circling minima is treated as a non-precision procedure**, irrespective of the type of instrument guidance used initially. During its final instrument segment, descent is limited to the published **minimum descent altitude or height for circling (MDA/H)**.

Circling may be necessary when the alignment of the instrument approach does not permit a suitable straight-in landing on the runway selected for touchdown.

## 9.2 Conduct of the Visual Manoeuvre

The circling phase is flown visually, with the runway environment continuously in view. Acceptable visual references may include the runway itself, its markings, runway lights and approach lighting.

There is no single prescribed flight path suitable for every circling approach. The manoeuvre depends on the aerodrome's runway arrangement, the direction of the preceding final approach, the wind and the prevailing meteorological conditions. After establishing the necessary visual reference, the aircraft is expected to remain at the applicable circling MDA/H until it can continue safely towards landing while retaining the runway environment in sight.

## 9.3 The Protected Visual Manoeuvring (Circling) Area

Visual manoeuvring is permitted only inside the established **VM(C) area**. Its boundaries are constructed separately for the different aircraft approach categories.

The procedure designer places arcs around the runway thresholds and connects them with lines tangent to the arcs. The arc radii depend on the manoeuvring characteristics of the aircraft, using these factors:

- **Aircraft approach category.**
- **Manoeuvring speed** applicable to that category.
- **Wind:** an assumed speed of **25 kt** throughout the turn.
- **Bank limitation:** **20° of bank or a rate-one turn**, whichever calls for the smaller bank angle.

These assumptions establish the space required for an aircraft to remain inside the protected area while circling.

## 9.4 Restricted or Prohibited Circling Sectors

A particular portion of a circling area may contain an obstacle that would force an impractically high minimum descent altitude or height. The designer may therefore exclude that sector from visual manoeuvring rather than impose its obstacle clearance requirement on the entire circling area.

Whenever this option is used, the published procedure identifies both the affected sector and the restriction. A pilot must not circle within a sector designated as prohibited.

## 9.5 Missed Approach During Circling

If the required visual reference is lost at any point during the circling manoeuvre, **a missed approach is required**. The applicable missed approach is the one published for the **instrument approach runway**, not a newly chosen route for the runway on which the pilot had intended to land.

The expected manoeuvre is a climbing turn towards the aerodrome/runway area, followed by establishment on the published missed approach track when the aircraft is in a suitable position over the aerodrome. While executing the circling manoeuvre, the pilot must therefore maintain the visual reference to the landing runway threshold; loss of that reference requires the missed approach rather than continuation of the circling pattern.

## 9.6 Circling Obstacle Clearance and Minimum Visibility

Circling minima are established for each aircraft category. The applicable **obstacle clearance altitude/height (OCA/H)** and minimum visibility are not necessarily the same for different categories because each category has its own visual manoeuvring area and speed requirements.

The **ICAO circling minima** are shown below. These values differ from the JAR-OPS minima used in Operational Procedures.

| Aircraft category | Obstacle clearance m (ft) | Lowest OCH above aerodrome elevation m (ft) | Minimum visibility km (NM) |
|:---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 90 (295) | 120 (394) | 1.9 (1.0) |
| B | 90 (295) | 150 (492) | 2.8 (1.5) |
| C | 120 (394) | 180 (591) | 3.7 (2.0) |
| D | 120 (394) | 210 (689) | 4.6 (2.5) |
| E | 150 (492) | 240 (787) | 6.5 (3.5) |
