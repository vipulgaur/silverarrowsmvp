# Chapter 10: Holding Procedures

## 10.1 Purpose and Use of Holding

A holding procedure allows an aircraft to remain within a designated volume of airspace while awaiting permission to proceed. Unlike an aircraft on the ground, a fixed-wing aeroplane cannot remain stationary. It therefore flies a defined, repeating pattern referenced to a radio navigation aid—such as a VOR or NDB—or an identified geographical/navigation fix.

Holding provides a way to absorb delays without losing an aircraft's position in the arrival sequence. It is particularly useful when poor weather or heavy traffic prevents aircraft from proceeding directly into an approach. Accurate aircraft control and navigation are essential because the aircraft must remain inside the protected holding airspace.

Instrument arrival arrangements use a holding pattern at the **initial approach fix (IAF)**. More than one aircraft may be assigned to the same holding location at different altitudes. In this arrangement, commonly described as a **stack**, aircraft are vertically separated by **1,000 ft**. When the lowest aircraft is released from the hold to begin its approach, the aircraft above can be cleared down individually into the vacated levels. Moving an aircraft upward or downward within a holding pattern is called a **shuttle**.

## 10.2 Keeping Within the Protected Area

Holding procedures are designed within boundaries established under **PANS-OPS**. Flying a different track or using an incorrect entry can take an aircraft outside the area for which obstacle protection has been assessed.

The standard entry and holding procedures are described for patterns using **right-hand turns**. When a hold has left-hand turns, the entry and flight paths are reflected about the inbound holding track: the result is a mirror image of the corresponding right-hand procedure.

## 10.3 Standard Pattern: Shape and Terms

A standard holding pattern consists of two generally parallel straight tracks linked by a turn at each end. Its standard direction of turn is **right**. An approach chart identifies a required left-hand pattern whenever one is specified.

The main elements are:

- **Holding fix or facility:** The navigation reference that defines the fix end of the pattern.
- **Fix end:** The end containing the holding fix, through which the aircraft passes on its inbound track.
- **Outbound end:** The opposite end, where the aircraft turns back toward the inbound track.
- **Inbound track:** The planned ground track leading toward the holding fix.
- **Outbound track:** The planned ground track flown away from the fix following the turn at the fix end.
- **Holding side:** The side of the inbound track on which the standard pattern is flown.
- **Non-holding side:** The opposite side of the inbound track.
- **Abeam position:** A position alongside the fix, used when establishing outbound timing.
- **Outbound time/distance (d):** The specified length of the outbound portion. For a time-based hold in still air, the reference period is **1 minute at 14,000 ft or below**, and **1½ minutes above 14,000 ft**.

## 10.4 Flying a Holding Pattern

### Turns and Track Accuracy

Every turn in the standard procedure is made at either **25° bank** or **rate one (3° per second)**, using **whichever requires the smaller bank angle**.

The published pattern specifies **tracks**, not headings. Pilots must therefore allow for the wind when selecting headings and calculating timings. These corrections apply both when joining the hold and when flying the established pattern.

### Outbound Timing and DME Limits

Start timing the outbound portion at the **later of** the following two events:

1. Passing over the holding fix.
2. Reaching the position abeam the fix.

If the abeam position cannot be established, begin timing **on completion of the outbound turn**.

For a time-defined outbound leg:

- At **14,000 ft and below**, use **1 minute**.
- Above **14,000 ft**, use **1½ minutes**.

Where the outbound portion is defined by a **DME limit** instead of time, finish the outbound leg when the specified limiting DME distance is reached.

If the pilot cannot comply with normal holding procedures for any reason, **ATC must be informed as soon as practicable**.

### Maximum Holding Speeds

The speed limits depend on altitude and on whether normal or turbulence conditions apply. The listed height bands may represent either altitudes or their corresponding flight levels, according to the altimeter setting being used.

| Holding level | Normal conditions | Turbulence conditions |
|---|---|---|
| Up to and including 4,250 m (14,000 ft) | 425 km/h (230 kt); or 315 km/h (170 kt) for Category A/B-only holds | 520 km/h (280 kt); or 315 km/h (170 kt) for Category A/B-only holds |
| Above 4,250 m (14,000 ft) to and including 6,100 m (20,000 ft) | 445 km/h (240 kt) | 520 km/h (280 kt) or Mach 0.8, whichever is less |
| Above 6,100 m (20,000 ft) to and including 10,350 m (34,000 ft) | 490 km/h (265 kt) | 520 km/h (280 kt) or Mach 0.8, whichever is less |
| Above 10,350 m (34,000 ft) | Mach 0.83 | Mach 0.83 |

Conditions attached to these limits:

1. The levels in the table are interpreted as altitudes or equivalent flight levels depending on the altimeter setting in use.
2. If an initial instrument-approach segment following the holding procedure is published at a speed greater than **425 km/h (230 kt)**, the hold should also be published at the higher speed wherever practicable.
3. The turbulence allowance of **520 km/h (280 kt)** or **Mach 0.8**, as applicable, is normally available **only with prior ATC clearance**, unless published information confirms that the holding area accommodates the higher speeds.
4. The **315 km/h (170 kt)** alternative applies only to patterns limited to **aircraft Categories A and B**.
5. Wherever practicable, holds associated with an airway-route structure should be designed for **520 km/h (280 kt)**.

## 10.5 Holding-Pattern Entry Sectors

The entry method is selected from the aircraft's approach direction relative to the holding fix and the inbound holding track. For a standard right-hand pattern there are **three entry sectors**:

- **Sector 1:** Parallel entry.
- **Sector 2:** Offset entry.
- **Sector 3:** Direct entry.

A **±5° tolerance** applies at each sector boundary. If the arrival heading lies within this band, the pilot may choose either of the adjacent entry procedures.

For a left-hand hold, the sectors and the associated manoeuvres are mirrored around the inbound track.

## 10.6 Sector 1 — Parallel Entry

After reaching the holding fix, the aircraft enters the parallel procedure as follows:

1. **Turn left** to establish an outbound track **reciprocal to the published inbound holding track**.
2. Maintain the reciprocal track for the outbound time appropriate to the aircraft's altitude.
3. **Turn left** to return toward the holding fix.
4. On crossing the fix a second time, **turn right** and follow the standard holding pattern.

## 10.7 Sector 2 — Offset Entry

The offset procedure begins at the holding fix:

1. Turn onto a track **30° to the left of the reciprocal of the published inbound holding track**.
2. Fly that diverging outbound track for the time appropriate to the holding altitude.
3. **Turn right** to re-intercept the holding inbound track and return to the fix.
4. At the next crossing of the fix, **turn right** to enter the normal holding pattern.

## 10.8 Sector 3 — Direct Entry

In the direct procedure, the aircraft reaches the fix and makes the required **right turn into the published holding pattern**. No preliminary parallel or offset manoeuvre is needed.

## 10.9 ATC Clearance to Join a Hold

Holding patterns used by controlled flights are normally inside controlled airspace. ATC, generally the approach controller, issues the clearance that permits the aircraft to join.

A joining clearance identifies:

- The **holding location/fix**.
- The **holding-pattern details**, when the procedure is not already routinely published.
- The **cleared holding level**.
- Any **special instructions or restrictions**.

A representative exchange is:

**ATC:** “G-CD hold at OX FL50 expected approach time 1020”  
**Pilot:** “Hold OX FL50 1020 G-CD”

After completing the entry and establishing the aircraft in the hold:

**Pilot:** “Oxford Approach G-CD is established in the hold at FL50”

The pilot must be level at the cleared holding level **at least 5 NM before the holding fix**. After acknowledging the initial clearance, no further report is expected during the join itself; the next report is made when the aircraft is established in the hold.

## 10.10 Changing Level in the Hold

When the next lower holding level is available, the controller may authorize the aircraft to descend into it. Once that clearance has been read back, the pilot begins the descent immediately.

A **shuttle** is a climb or descent carried out while flying a holding pattern.

Representative radio exchange:

**ATC:** “G-CD shuttle in the hold to FL40”  
**Pilot:** “Leaving FL50 descending to FL40 G-CD”

On reaching the cleared level:

**Pilot:** “G-CD level FL40”

## 10.11 Leaving the Holding Pattern

When the arrival sequence permits, ATC clears an aircraft out of the hold and onto the instrument approach. Normally the aircraft leaves the pattern **at the holding fix**. With radar control, an approach radar controller may instead provide vectors from **any location in the holding pattern**.

A representative sequence is:

**ATC:** “G-CD advise when ready to commence the procedure”  
**Pilot:** “Ready to commence the procedure G-CD”

**ATC:** “G-CD set the Oxford QNH 1003, clear NDB/DME approach runway 01, report turning inbound at 2000 ft”  
**Pilot:** “1003 set clear NDB/DME runway 01 wilco G-CD”

For **timed approaches**, the controller may specify the time at which the aircraft is to leave the hold. The pilot adjusts the holding leg lengths so that the aircraft passes the fix and commences the procedure as nearly as possible to the cleared departure time.

## 10.12 Holding-Area Obstacle Clearance

The protected holding volume must accommodate both the established racetrack and the surrounding airspace required for entry manoeuvres. The procedure designer applies minimum obstacle clearance (**MOC**) throughout the holding area:

- **300 m** under the stated normal-terrain criterion.
- **600 m** in mountainous terrain.

The extent of the holding area depends on the published pattern, the aircraft types expected to use it, any adjacent-airspace constraints and the highest holding level.

Outside the defined holding area is a **5 NM-wide buffer area**. Obstacle-clearance protection within this buffer decreases progressively, from the full MOC at the holding-area boundary to **zero at the outer edge**.
