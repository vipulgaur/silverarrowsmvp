# Chapter 23 — Facilitation

## Aim

### 23.1 Article 37

Annex 9 contains the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) Standards and Recommended Practices (SARPs) concerning facilitation. Their foundation is **Article 37 of the Convention on International Civil Aviation**, which empowers ICAO to establish and periodically revise international standards, recommended practices and procedures. This authority covers customs and immigration arrangements, together with other subjects affecting the safety, regularity and efficiency of international air navigation.

Two further Convention provisions establish the corresponding responsibilities of Contracting States:

- **Article 22** requires States to take all practicable steps, whether by specific regulations or other measures, to make aircraft movements between Contracting States easier and faster. They are to avoid unnecessary delays affecting aircraft, crew, passengers and cargo, particularly those arising from immigration, quarantine, customs and clearance controls.
- **Article 23** commits each State, to the extent practicable, to bring its customs and immigration procedures for international aviation into line with the practices established or recommended under the Convention.

### 23.2 Documentation

The papers historically used to authorize the international arrival and departure of aircraft, their crews and their passengers developed from shipping documents; consequently, many maritime terms remain in aviation use. However, the much greater speed of air transport created a need to reduce administrative delays. Traditional documentation has therefore become obsolete in some applications, while in others it has been superseded by electronic records and digital transmission or exchange of information.

## Entry and Departure of Aircraft

### 23.3 General

Procedures and government requirements for clearing an aircraft must provide treatment at least as favourable as that given to other transport modes. Each Contracting State must arrange aircraft-clearance formalities so that aviation-security checks and suitable narcotics-control measures can be performed without forfeiting the speed advantage of air travel.

### 23.4 Documents

For aircraft entry and departure, public authorities may request from operators only the documents specified in **ICAO Annex 9**. If a State adopts electronic data interchange (**EDI**) for a particular clearance task, its authorities should plan a transition to complete electronic exchange of the necessary information, progressively eliminating paper preparation and paper-document transfers.

Where the destination State permits a person to enter without a visa, as in a visa-waiver arrangement, the State of departure must not impose a requirement to obtain an identity document beyond the traveller's passport.

### 23.5 General Declaration

A **General Declaration** is an internationally accepted form recording key particulars of an aircraft and its journey. It identifies the aircraft by its nationality and registration markings, states the flight number and date, and records departure and destination details. Its route information includes the crew and passenger numbers associated with embarking and disembarking at the relevant stops. The form also contains a declaration of health and a certification signed by the pilot-in-command or an authorized representative. The longer-term intention is to remove references to passengers from the General Declaration.

A State must not insist on receiving a General Declaration when it can obtain the requisite information readily through another acceptable method. Where the form remains mandatory, the authorities must accept a signature by either the **pilot-in-command** or the **authorized agent**. If the agent signing the overall declaration is not a crew member, authorities may separately require the health declaration to bear the signature of a crew member.

Information demanded specifically about crew on an arriving or departing aircraft is restricted to the **number of crew members on board**. If this is reported on a General Declaration, the appropriate entry is the field headed **“Total number of crew”**.

### 23.6 Manifests

Two other internationally recognized aviation documents are the **Passenger Manifest** and **Cargo Manifest**. The former lists passenger names; the latter records goods and the number of packages loaded on the aircraft.

If a Contracting State no longer requires a Passenger Manifest and does not require a General Declaration except as proof or attestation, the operator must be allowed to choose between supplying a General Declaration and supplying an appropriate signed attestation on **a single page of the Cargo Manifest**. The authorized agent or the pilot-in-command may sign that attestation, which may also be applied using a rubber stamp.

The Cargo Manifest provides the **Air Waybill number**, the **number of packages** and the **nature of the goods**.

### 23.7 Passenger Baggage

Contracting States must not demand a separate statement showing the number of pieces of **accompanied baggage**. When authorities request baggage information that the passenger has not already supplied for customs clearance, the operator must make any available relevant information accessible to them. A simplified customs-clearance procedure may be used for **unaccompanied baggage**.

### 23.8 Stores List

The **Stores List** identifies articles carried for consumption or use by passengers and crew, whether or not those articles are offered for sale. It also covers supplies needed for aircraft operation or servicing, including **fuel and lubricants**.

### 23.9 Unaccompanied Baggage

Although unaccompanied baggage is subject to the procedures applicable to **cargo**, its clearance is to be conducted using a **simplified process**.

### 23.10 Oral Declaration

The contents of crew or passenger baggage may be declared **orally**. Such a declaration is acceptable, and baggage may be subjected to **random inspection**.

### 23.11 Completion of Documents

Required documentation may be prepared by typing, as electronically generated data, or by handwriting with **ink or an indelible pencil**. Handwritten entries must remain clearly legible and use **block capitals**.

### 23.12 Number and Type of Documents Required

A Contracting State must not demand from an aircraft operator more than the following number of copies:

| Document | Maximum copies |
|---|---:|
| General Declaration | 3 |
| Cargo Manifest | 3 |
| General Stores List | 3 |

### 23.13 Advanced Notification of Arrival

For a **non-scheduled flight** by an aircraft registered in an ICAO Contracting State, where the aircraft intends to land in another Contracting State **solely for non-traffic purposes** (a **second-freedom flight**), filing a flight plan provides sufficient advance notification of the operation.

Acceptance on that basis is subject to **both** of these conditions: the receiving State obtains the flight plan **at least 2 hours before the aircraft arrives**, and the aircraft lands at an **airport previously designated as an international airport**. If advance notification is required by particular national bodies—for example, customs, immigration or police—the flight plan must be addressed to the applicable authorities in that State.

### 23.14 Crew and Other Operators' Personnel

Where a Contracting State inspects crew members or their baggage when they enter or leave, it must complete those inspections as promptly as possible. States must also provide arrangements enabling crew employed by their national airlines to acquire **Crew Member Certificates (CMCs)** without delay and **free of charge**. A CMC remains valid throughout the member's period of employment.

The CMC serves as an identity card for flight crew and cabin attendants. This avoids using flight crew licences as routine identification documents, allowing the licences to retain their main role as proof of professional flying qualifications.

A State must accept a CMC in place of a passport or visa for **temporary entry** by airline flight crew and cabin attendants when all the relevant conditions are satisfied: the individual holds the CMC while embarking and disembarking; remains at the airport of the intermediate stop or within an adjacent city; and subsequently departs either on the same aircraft or on that person's next regularly scheduled flight.

This recognition is intended to apply even if a crew member is **not a national of the aircraft's State of Registry**. States are not discouraged from issuing CMCs to resident foreign-national crew members. The arrangement is intended to help airlines deploy personnel efficiently and promptly; its overall advantage is diminished when some States decline to recognize the certificates.

### 23.15 Non-scheduled Operations

Contracting States must extend temporary-entry privileges to flight crew and cabin attendants operating aircraft **for hire or remuneration**, but outside **scheduled international air services**. This concession is conditional upon the crew members leaving the State **on that aircraft's first outbound flight**.

### 23.16 Transit Passengers

A passenger may remain within a **transit lounge for no more than 2 days**.

### 23.17 Freedom from Duty

An aircraft that is **not operating a scheduled international air service**, and is flying to or via a designated airport in a Contracting State, may be granted **temporary admission free of duty**. If so admitted, it may remain in the State for the duration specified by that State.

### 23.18 Responsibility of the Operator for Custody and Care of Passengers

An operator's responsibility for the custody and care of passengers and crew members **ends when those persons have been admitted into the State**.
