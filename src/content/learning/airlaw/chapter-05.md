# Chapter 5: Flight Crew Licensing

## Introduction

### 5.1 Licensing Framework and Examination Requirements

Flight crew licensing is governed internationally by **ICAO Annex 1 — Personnel Licensing** and, in the European framework covered here, by the **Joint Aviation Requirements for Flight Crew Licensing (JAR-FCL)**. The Air Law syllabus includes ICAO Standards and Recommended Practices (SARPs), while the question bank also examines the particular JAR-FCL provisions. Where ICAO and JAA arrangements differ, each set of requirements needs to be distinguished.

### 5.2 National and International Licensing Standards

Under **Article 38 of the Chicago Convention**, a Contracting State notifies ICAO when its national practices differ from an Annex. Such differences appear in a supplement to the relevant Annex. The supplement to **Annex 1** is particularly extensive, reflecting the variation in licensing and medical standards between States.

Licensing arrangements applied by organizations and authorities such as the **FAA, JAA, EASA and UK CAA** have not been identical to the licensing and medical provisions in Annex 1. A licence holder must therefore follow the rules of the actual licensing authority rather than assuming that the ICAO Annex provisions alone determine licence issue, renewal or continued validity.

### 5.3 European JAR-FCL Divisions

The JAR-FCL system divides licensing matters into four parts:

| Part | Subject |
|---|---|
| **JAR-FCL 1** | Aeroplane pilot licensing |
| **JAR-FCL 2** | Helicopter pilot licensing |
| **JAR-FCL 3** | Medical requirements for aircrew licensing |
| **JAR-FCL 4** | Flight engineer licensing, including systems-panel operators |

The transition described in this framework is from JAR-FCL to **EASA Part-FCL** and **Part-MED**.

## Definitions Used in Flight Crew Licensing

### 5.4–5.9 Flight Crew and Flight Activity

**5.4 — Annex 1 definitions.** The following licensing terms have defined meanings within the ICAO personnel-licensing provisions.

**5.5 — Pilot-in-command (PIC).** The pilot accountable during flight time for aircraft safety and adherence to the Rules of the Air.

**5.6 — Aircraft category.** A broad grouping determined by an aircraft's principal characteristics, such as **aeroplane, helicopter, glider or free balloon**.

**5.7 — Commander.** The pilot selected by the operator to command the flight. This person is qualified to act as PIC and may entrust the conduct of the flight to another appropriately qualified pilot.

**5.8 — Co-pilot.** A licensed pilot performing piloting duties other than those of the PIC. A pilot carried exclusively to receive instruction is not included in this definition.

**5.9 — Cross-country flight.** A journey along a planned route between a departure point and an arrival point, using accepted navigation methods.

### 5.10–5.16 Flying Time, Ratings and Training Devices

**5.10 — Dual instruction time.** Flying time during which an authorized instructor on board provides instruction to a trainee.

**5.11 — Instrument flight time.** Time spent flying an aircraft using instruments alone, without relying on outside visual references.

**5.12 — Flight time.** The period beginning when an aircraft initially moves under its own power with the intention of taking off and ending when it finally stops after the flight. This is commonly described as **block-to-block** or **chock-to-chock** time.

**5.13 — Instrument ground time.** Time spent practising simulated instrument flight in a ground-based flight-training device accepted by the licensing authority.

**5.14 — Instrument time.** The combined category encompassing instrument flight time and instrument ground time.

**5.15 — Rating.** A qualification forming part of, or linked to, a pilot licence which states additional authorizations and any conditions or restrictions on their use.

**5.16 — Synthetic flight trainer.** Ground equipment designed to reproduce flight conditions. The three forms identified are:

- **Flight simulator:** A detailed representation of a particular aircraft's flight deck, controls, mechanical and electrical/electronic systems, operating environment, handling and performance.
- **Flight procedures trainer:** A device reproducing a realistic flight-deck environment, instrument indications, elementary system and control responses, and the performance and flight characteristics of an aircraft class.
- **Basic instrument flight trainer:** A training installation with appropriate flight instruments which recreates the cockpit environment encountered when flying on instruments.

## General Licensing Rules

### 5.17 Authority to Perform Flight-Crew Duties

A person may act as a flight-crew member only when appropriately licensed for the intended duties. Under the Annex 1 framework, the licence must originate from the aircraft's **State of Registry** or be issued by another Contracting State and **accepted as valid by the State of Registry**.

### 5.18 Re-issue of a JAA Licence

A JAA pilot licence has a **five-year** document-validity period. During that period the licensing authority may reissue it:

- Following the original issue or renewal of a rating.
- When **paragraph XII** of the licence has no remaining space.
- For an administrative requirement.
- If the JAA State's authority elects to reissue it during rating revalidation.

### 5.19 Recognition and Validation of Licences

A Contracting State can recognize a foreign licence by issuing a validation, but that validation cannot continue after the underlying licence has expired. Under the JAR-FCL arrangement described here, a JAA-issued licence and its associated ratings are accepted by other JAA member States without additional formal validation.

### 5.20 Non-JAA Licences Used in JAA States

A foreign licence containing an **instrument rating** may receive validation for operation in a JAA State for a maximum of **one year**, provided the original licence continues to be valid in its issuing State.

### 5.21 Credit for Qualifications and Experience

When assessing an application for an equivalent JAA licence, the relevant member-State authority can recognize some of the applicant's previous **theoretical training** and/or **flying experience** gained under a non-JAA licence. The amount of credit is decided by that authority.

### 5.22 Scope of Licence Privileges

A State-issued pilot licence permits operation in accordance with its authorizations for aircraft registered in that State. Neither the licence nor an associated rating permits a holder to exercise privileges beyond those explicitly granted.

### 5.23 Medical Fitness and Substances

Pilots must hold the required medical certification: **Annex 1, Chapter 6** supplies the international medical provisions, while **JAR-FCL 3** governs the JAA arrangements considered here. A licence holder must stop exercising licence privileges when aware that physical or psychological fitness has declined.

Flying under the influence of a **psychoactive substance** is prohibited, as is problematic substance use. A pilot considering prolonged medication should obtain advice from an approved **Aeromedical Examiner (AME)**.

### 5.24 Ongoing Validity

A licence's privileges depend not only on the licence document itself but also on the validity of its **ratings, certificates and medical assessment**. The pilot must continue to satisfy competence and recent-experience conditions applicable to the activity.

### 5.25 Recent Flying Experience

A pilot must satisfy the following recency requirements before operating in **commercial air transport** or carrying passengers:

- **By day, as PIC or co-pilot:** Within the preceding **90 days**, the pilot must have completed at least **three take-offs, approaches and landings** in the relevant aircraft type or class, or in a **full flight simulator (FFS)** representing it.
- **As PIC at night:** Within the previous **90 days**, the pilot must have completed at least **one take-off, approach and landing at night as pilot flying**, using the relevant aircraft or appropriate FFS, **or hold an instrument rating**.

### 5.26–5.27 Currency of Instrument Ratings

**5.26 — Ratings.** Where flying privileges depend on a rating, that rating must be current; prescribed testing or revalidation is required to keep the privileges available.

**5.27 — Instrument rating (IR).** The IR remains valid for **12 months**. Revalidation can be undertaken during its final **three months**. Under the stated JAR-FCL rule, failing the renewal instrument-rating test causes the existing IR to be cancelled.

### 5.28 Normal Residency

For the JAA residency requirement, a person is regarded as normally resident in a JAA country when resident there for **185 days in a year**.

### 5.29 Class- and Type-Rating Validity

| Rating | Validity |
|---|---|
| Multi-engine class rating | **12 months** |
| Type rating | **12 months** |
| Single-pilot, single-engine class rating, including touring motor gliders | **2 years** |

A rating normally runs from its issue date. When it is revalidated before it expires, the next validity period is calculated from the previous expiry date. Renewal involves a **pilot proficiency / skills test**.

### 5.30 Medical Report Intervals

The medical examination intervals are differentiated by licence and operation:

| Licence / medical category | Interval prescribed in the ICAO/JAA framework described |
|---|---|
| **CPL(A) / ATPL(A), Class 1** | **12 months** until age **60**; **6 months** thereafter. For single-pilot commercial operations, the **six-month** interval begins at age **40**. |
| **MPL, Class 1 (ICAO)** | **12 months** until age **60**; **6 months** thereafter. |
| **PPL(A), Class 2 (ICAO)** | **60 months** until age **40**; **24 months** afterward; a **12-month** interval is recommended beyond age **50**. |
| **PPL(A), Class 2 (JAA)** | **60 months** until age **40**; **24 months** from age **40 to 50**; **12 months** after age **50**. |

### 5.31 Deferment of a Medical Examination

**Annex 1** allows certain medical-assessment deferrals when a pilot is operating in an isolated area where suitable medical facilities are unavailable, subject to the authority's approval:

- Up to **six months** for flight crew not conducting commercial aviation.
- Up to **two consecutive periods of three months** for commercial-air-transport flight crew, provided the required satisfactory report is obtained from a qualified examiner or, if no examiner is available, a practising physician. The report must reach the licence-issuing State's authority.
- A single period of up to **24 months** for a private pilot.

This **ICAO deferment facility does not apply to JAA pilots**: JAR-FCL 3 requires their medical certification to remain current.

### 5.32–5.33 Age Restrictions in Commercial Air Transport

**5.32 — Age 60:** A pilot aged **60 or more** may act in commercial air transport only as part of a **multi-pilot crew**, and only when no other pilot in that crew has reached 60.

**5.33 — Age 65:** On reaching **65**, a pilot may no longer act as a pilot in commercial air transport under the arrangements described. Some JAA States apply a lower upper age of **60**.

## Pilot Licences and Ratings

### 5.34 Licence Categories

To operate as PIC or co-pilot, a person requires a licence covering the relevant category of aircraft. The categories are **aeroplane, helicopter, glider and free balloon**.

### 5.35 Levels and Designations of Pilot Licences

The licence types discussed are:

- **Private Pilot Licence (PPL)**
- **Commercial Pilot Licence (CPL)**
- **Air Transport Pilot Licence (ATPL)**
- **Multi-crew Pilot Licence (MPL)**

The category is indicated in brackets after the licence designation; for example, **CPL(A)** identifies a commercial pilot licence for aeroplanes.

### 5.36 Aeroplane Class Ratings

For aeroplanes certified for **single-pilot** operation, the basic class-rating divisions are:

- Single-engine land aeroplanes.
- Single-engine sea aeroplanes.
- Multi-engine land aeroplanes.
- Multi-engine sea aeroplanes.

### 5.37 Additional JAR-FCL Class Ratings

Further class-rating groupings include **touring motor gliders**, and **single-engine turboprop land or sea aeroplanes grouped by manufacturer**.

### 5.38 Issue and Holding of Type Ratings

A pilot receives a type rating after completing an approved course for the aircraft type and demonstrating the required handling skills. A rating restricting the pilot to **co-pilot duties** must carry an appropriate endorsement.

There is **no fixed numerical limit** on the number of class and type ratings a pilot may hold concurrently. However, the authority advises pilots to retain only as many as they can safely maintain as current and competent.

### 5.39 Criteria Used to Establish Separate Type Ratings

The JAR-FCL assessment uses the aircraft's:

1. Airworthiness **type certificate**.
2. **Handling characteristics**.
3. Certified **minimum flight-crew complement**.
4. **Technology level**.

### 5.40 Aircraft Requiring a Type Rating

A separate type rating is required for:

- Every **multi-pilot aeroplane type**.
- Every **single-pilot, multi-engine** type equipped with **turbojet or turboprop** engines.
- Every **single-pilot, single-engine** type equipped with a **turbojet** engine.
- Any additional type for which the authority requires an individual type rating.

### 5.41 Type Ratings Under ICAO Annex 1

The international provisions specifically identify:

- Each **multi-pilot aircraft type**.
- Each **single-pilot helicopter type**.
- Helicopters approved for single-pilot operation that are **not included in a class rating**.

### 5.42 Training for Aircraft Variants

An individual variant within an existing type rating may require **differences training**. If the pilot has not flown that variant for **two years**, differences training must be completed again.

### 5.43 Prerequisites for a Multi-pilot Aeroplane Type Rating

Under JAR-FCL, the candidate must have:

- At least **100 hours as PIC of aeroplanes**.
- A valid **multi-engine IR(A)**.
- A **multi-crew co-operation (MCC)** certificate.
- Completion of the **ATPL(A) theoretical knowledge course** and success in its examinations.

These requirements apply irrespective of the pilot-licence level already held.

### 5.44 Synthetic Devices in Skill Tests

A licensing authority may approve the use of an appropriate synthetic flight-training device when an applicant demonstrates manoeuvres required for issue of a licence or rating. The device must be suitable for the particular test purpose.

### 5.45 Instrument Ratings and IFR Privileges

A licence holder normally requires a valid **instrument rating (IR)** for the appropriate aircraft category before acting as pilot or co-pilot under **instrument flight rules (IFR)**. JAR-FCL also recognizes that a State may establish national permissions for restricted IFR operation without a full IR(A), such as the former **UK IMC rating**. Such permissions are confined to the national airspace to which they apply.

### 5.46 Authorization to Instruct

A licensed pilot cannot provide flight instruction toward the issue of a pilot licence or rating without the **specific instructor authorization** required by the licensing State.

### 5.47 Credit of Flight Time

Time eligible to count toward a pilot licence includes:

- **Full credit** for the applicant's **solo, dual-instruction and PIC** flight time when working toward initial issue or a more advanced licence.
- Up to **100% credit** for flight time spent as required co-pilot on an aeroplane in which a co-pilot is mandatory.
- **Full credit** for co-pilot flight time during which the pilot performs **PIC duties under supervision (PICUS)** toward a higher-grade licence.

### 5.48 Student Pilot Supervision

States must ensure student pilots do not create a hazard to air navigation. Solo flight is permitted only with an authorized instructor's **permission or supervision**. International solo flights require an agreement between the Contracting States concerned. A student must be at least **16 years old** and hold a **Class 2 medical assessment or better** before flying solo.

## Private Pilot Licence — Aeroplane, PPL(A)

### 5.49 Age

A PPL(A) applicant must be at least **17** to obtain the licence. Training may commence earlier, but the minimum age for student solo flight remains **16**.

### 5.50 Knowledge

The applicant must satisfy the prescribed **theoretical knowledge examination** requirements.

### 5.51 Flying Experience

A PPL(A) applicant must complete at least **45 flight hours**, including:

- No more than **five hours** of approved synthetic-training time credited toward the required total.
- At least **10 hours of solo flight**.
- Within the solo total, at least **five hours of solo cross-country**.
- A solo cross-country flight of **at least 270 km (150 NM)** with a **full-stop landing at two different aerodromes**.

With approval, flying experience from another aircraft category may receive credit.

### 5.52 Medical Standard

A valid **Class 2 medical assessment** is required.

### 5.53 PPL(A) Privileges

A PPL(A) holder may act as **PIC or co-pilot** of an aeroplane on **non-commercial / non-revenue flights**. Exercising these privileges at night requires appropriate dual night instruction covering **take-offs, landings and navigation**.

## Commercial Pilot Licence — Aeroplane, CPL(A)

### 5.54 Minimum Age

The applicant must be **18 years** old or older.

### 5.55 Knowledge Subjects

A CPL(A) candidate must demonstrate the specified knowledge standard by examination in:

- Air Law.
- Aircraft general knowledge.
- Flight performance and planning.
- Human performance and limitations.
- Meteorology.
- Navigation.
- Operational procedures.
- Principles of flight.
- Radiotelephony.

### 5.56 Flight-Time Requirements

The standard experience requirement is **at least 200 hours**, with up to **10 hours** of approved synthetic-training time included where authorized. An approved **integrated CPL(A)** course instead requires at least **150 hours**, including progress tests, and may include **up to five hours of instrument ground time** within that integrated course.

The specified experience elements are:

- At least **100 hours as PIC**, or **70 hours** in an approved course.
- **20 hours of cross-country flight**, including one flight of **at least 540 km (300 NM)** with full-stop landings at **two separate aerodromes**.
- **10 hours of instrument instruction**, of which at most **five hours** may be on the ground.
- Where night privileges are sought, **five hours of night flying**, including **five take-offs and five landings as PIC**.

Recognized flight time from other aircraft categories can be credited with authority permission.

### 5.57 Medical Standard

A current **Class 1 medical assessment** is required.

### 5.58 Privileges of the CPL(A)

A CPL(A) includes the privileges of a PPL(A). In addition, subject to appropriate ratings and restrictions, the holder may:

- Act as **PIC on aeroplane operations other than commercial air transport**.
- Act as **PIC in commercial air transport** when the aeroplane is certificated for **single-pilot operation**.
- Act as **co-pilot in commercial air transport** on aeroplanes for which a co-pilot is required.

For multi-pilot commercial-air-transport type-rating training, the JAR-FCL arrangement requires prior success in the **ATPL(A) theoretical knowledge examinations**. Night privileges also depend on dual instruction that includes night take-offs, landings and navigation.

## Air Transport Pilot Licence — Aeroplane, ATPL(A)

### 5.59 Minimum Age

The applicant must be at least **21**.

### 5.60 Theory Examinations

The subject areas are those specified for the CPL(A), but the ATPL(A) requires a **higher level of theoretical knowledge**.

### 5.61 Experience and Multi-crew Competence

An ATPL(A) applicant must have at least **1,500 hours** of flight experience and demonstrate competence to fly **multi-crew aeroplanes under IFR**.

Approved synthetic training may contribute **up to 100 hours** to the overall experience; of this, no more than **25 hours** may be from a **flight procedures trainer or basic instrument trainer**.

The minimum experience includes:

- **500 hours as PIC under supervision (PICUS)**, or **250 hours** made up of PIC time (at least **70 hours actual PIC**) and further PICUS time.
- **200 hours cross-country**, including at least **100 hours** as PIC or co-pilot performing PIC duties under approved supervision.
- **75 hours instrument time**, with **no more than 30 hours** as instrument ground time.
- **100 hours night flight time** as PIC or co-pilot.
- Under JAR-FCL, an additional requirement of **500 hours in multi-pilot operations** involving transport-category or commuter-category aeroplanes, or an equivalent code.

Credit for piloting other aircraft categories may be granted by the authority.

### 5.62 Medical Standard

An ATPL(A) holder requires a current **Class 1 medical assessment**.

### 5.63 ATPL(A) Privileges

An ATPL(A) holder may use the privileges of the **PPL(A), CPL(A) and IR(A)**, and may act as **PIC or co-pilot on aeroplanes in commercial air transport**, subject to the appropriate aircraft qualifications.

## Multi-crew Pilot Licence — MPL

### 5.64 Purpose of the MPL

The MPL is intended for a pilot entering commercial air transport as a **co-pilot in an aeroplane requiring two or more pilots**. The associated training pathway prepares the pilot directly for multi-crew co-pilot duties rather than first following a conventional PIC-oriented CPL/ATPL training route.

### 5.65 Limitation

An MPL alone does **not** confer piloting privileges on **single-pilot aeroplanes**.

### 5.66 General Specification

The general MPL specification follows the concept of the JAA **“frozen ATPL(A)”** training and qualification pathway.

## Instrument Rating — Aeroplane, IR(A)

### 5.67 Knowledge and Skill Requirements

The knowledge examination for an IR(A) concentrates on operations conducted under **IFR**. Applicants also demonstrate the relevant instrument-handling skills. Where the privilege involves **multi-engine aeroplanes**, the assessment includes flying **solely by instruments with one engine inoperative**. An approved synthetic trainer may be used when permitted.

### 5.68 Eligibility and Prior Experience

The applicant must hold either a **PPL(A) with night qualification** or a **CPL(A)**. Before issue, at least **50 hours of cross-country time as PIC** must have been completed in aeroplanes or helicopters, including **not less than 10 hours in aeroplanes**.

### 5.69 Medical Conditions for PPL Holders

A private pilot seeking an IR(A) must satisfy the **Class 1 hearing requirement**. Contracting States are also advised to consider requiring the physical, mental and visual standards of Class 1.

### 5.70 Privileges of an IR(A)

When the underlying licence and medical remain appropriate and valid, the IR(A) authorizes flying **aeroplanes under IFR**. For pilots holding both helicopter and aeroplane licences, the licensing framework allows a **single instrument rating** to confer the relevant IFR privileges for both categories where applicable.

### 5.71 National Night and IMC Permissions

National provisions may permit a PPL(A) or CPL(A) holder without a full IR(A) to obtain:

- A **night rating** for flight in **VMC at night under IFR** where the State's arrangements require that form of permission.
- A restricted **IMC rating** allowing certain operations in meteorological conditions below VMC **outside controlled airspace**, potentially including nationally specified exemptions for certain control zones (CTRs).

Such authorizations depend on the relevant State's rules.

## Instructor and Examiner Ratings

### 5.72 Aeroplane Instructor Categories

Six instructor qualifications are identified:

| Abbreviation | Rating |
|---|---|
| **FI(A)** | Flight Instructor — Aeroplane |
| **TRI(A)** | Type Rating Instructor — Aeroplane |
| **CRI(A)** | Class Rating Instructor — Aeroplane |
| **IRI(A)** | Instrument Rating Instructor — Aeroplane |
| **SFI(A)** | Synthetic Flight Instructor — Aeroplane |
| **MCCI(A)** | Multi-crew Co-operation Course Instructor — Aeroplane |

### 5.73 Instructor Qualifications

An instructor must hold at least the licence, qualification or rating for which training is delivered and must be entitled to act as **PIC** during the instructional flight.

### 5.74 Instructor Rating Validity

Instructor ratings are valid for **three years**.

### 5.75 FI(A) Initial Restrictions

The minimum age for an FI(A) is **18 years**. A new instructor operates under supervision until both of the following have been accomplished:

- At least **100 hours of flight instruction** given.
- Supervision of **25 student solo flights**.

### 5.76 FI(A) Training-Course Entry Requirements

Before joining the FI(A) course, an applicant must meet these conditions:

- Hold a **CPL(A)**; alternatively, when holding a **PPL(A)**, have **200 hours total flight time**, with **150 hours as PIC**.
- Possess **CPL(A)-level theoretical knowledge**.
- Have at least **30 hours** in **single-engine piston aeroplanes**, including **five hours within the preceding six months**.
- Have received at least **10 hours of instrument instruction**, with at most **five hours of instrument ground time**.
- Have completed **20 hours of cross-country flight as PIC**, with the cross-country distance and landing standard used for a CPL(A).
- Pass the **pre-entry flight test**.

### 5.77 Unrestricted FI(A) Instructional Privileges

When the relevant experience requirements of **JAR-FCL 1.330** are met, an FI(A) can provide instruction toward:

- Issue of a **PPL(A)**.
- **Single-engine aeroplane class and type ratings**.
- Issue of a **CPL(A)**, for which the stated experience threshold is **500 hours total flying**, including **200 hours of instruction given**.
- **Night flying**, if holding the appropriate night qualification.
- **Single-engine and multi-engine IR(A)** qualifications.
- **Single-pilot multi-engine** class or type ratings.
- Issue of an **FI(A)** rating.

### 5.78 Examiner Categories

Five examiner roles are recognized:

- **FE(A)** — Flight Examiner.
- **TRE(A) / SFE(A)** — Type Rating Examiner / Synthetic Flight Examiner.
- **CRE(A)** — Class Rating Examiner.
- **IRE(A)** — Instrument Rating Examiner.
- **FIE(A)** — Flight Instructor Examiner.

### 5.79 Examiner Qualifications

An examiner is normally required to hold a licence and rating at least equivalent to the one being examined, as well as the authority to instruct toward that qualification unless a specific exception applies.

### 5.80 Examiner Authorization Period

Examiner authorization lasts **no longer than three years**, with subsequent reauthorization determined by the licensing authority.

## Medical Certification — JAR-FCL 3

### 5.81 Medical Certification Requirement

A person applying for or using pilot-licence privileges must possess the **JAR-FCL 3 medical certificate** applicable to those privileges. The pilot must be medically capable, both **physically and mentally**, of safely performing the duties authorized by the licence.

### 5.82 JAR-FCL Medical Classes

Two medical classes are prescribed for pilots:

- **Class 1:** CPL and ATPL holders.
- **Class 2:** PPL holders.

### 5.83 ICAO Medical Classes

ICAO's medical assessment classifications include:

- **Class 1:** Commercial pilots, flight engineers and flight navigators.
- **Class 2:** Private pilots, including glider and free-balloon pilots.
- **Class 3:** Air traffic controllers.

### 5.84 Medical Examination Results and Restrictions

Following examination, an applicant is advised whether the result is **fit**, **unfit** or **referred to the authority**. The AME must explain any medical or operational restrictions affecting training or the use of a licence. If PIC privileges are permitted only with a **safety pilot**, the authority supplies guidance to help establish that safety pilot's duties and responsibilities.

### 5.85 Periodic Examination

Regular medical examinations assess continuing fitness as a pilot grows older. Within the JAA provisions described here, there is **no general extension or deferment** of medical validity in place of the required examination.

### 5.86 Circumstances Requiring Medical Advice

A pilot who knows that fitness has deteriorated must refrain from using licence privileges and seek advice from the authority or AME. Particular circumstances listed are:

- Admission to hospital or a clinic for **more than 12 hours**.
- A **surgical operation or invasive procedure**.
- **Regular use of medication**.
- A requirement for **regular corrective-lens use**.

### 5.87 Operational Multi-crew Limitation (OML)

Where a commercial pilot cannot meet all Class 1 criteria, the medical may carry an **OML**, restricting the pilot to operation **“as or with qualified co-pilot.”** The accompanying pilot must be **qualified on type**, **younger than 60**, and **not subject to an OML**.

### 5.88 Conditions Requiring Notification

A licence holder must notify the authority in writing in cases of:

- **Serious injury** preventing normal flight-crew duties.
- An **illness continuing for 21 days or more** which prevents those duties; notification is due once the **21-day period** has elapsed.
- **Pregnancy**.

The medical certificate is regarded as **suspended** when one of these circumstances occurs. Following injury or illness, normal privileges are restored when the pilot is medically assessed as fit, unless the authority exempts the pilot from examination. During pregnancy, the authority may permit a limited period of flying under imposed conditions; unrestricted reinstatement follows the required post-pregnancy medical determination.

### 5.89 Effect of Medical-Certificate Suspension

Prompt notification is required following injury, diagnosis of pregnancy, or the **21st day** of prolonged illness. Suspension continues until the authority accepts the pilot as fit again. When the prescribed notification procedure is observed, the certificate's **unused period of validity is retained** during suspension and becomes available after reinstatement.

A pregnant pilot may be allowed to continue flight duties for a period considered medically acceptable; after delivery, restoration of unrestricted fitness requires the appropriate examination and assessment.

### 5.90 Renewal After Medical-Certificate Expiry

Medical validity begins on the date of the **initial general medical examination**. Where a certificate has expired, the examination requirements depend on the length of the lapse:

| Time expired | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Less than 90 days** | Standard or extended examination as prescribed. |
| **More than 90 days but less than 2 years** | Standard or extended examination at an **Aeromedical Centre (AMC)** or by an **AME authorized by the Aeromedical Section (AMS)**. |
| **More than 2 years but less than 5 years** | Standard or extended examination at an AMC or appropriately authorized AME, with previous medical records available. |
| **More than 5 years** | Initial or extended examination, at the AME's discretion, conducted by an AMC with access to the holder's medical records. |

### 5.91 The 45-Day Medical Revalidation Rule

When the revalidation examination takes place **not earlier than 45 days before expiry**, the new period of validity begins on the **previous certificate's expiry date** rather than on the examination date.

For example, if a certificate expires on **31 December**, an annual examination on or after **15 November** will support renewal covering **1 January–31 December** of the following year. If the examination is instead taken on **1 November**, the certificate's new period runs from **1 November to 31 October** of the next year.

## Pilot Proficiency

### 5.92 Recurrent Proficiency Checks

Pilots must periodically show that they retain the flying skills and emergency-procedure handling required for their duties. Where flying under **IFR** is involved, checks must take place **twice each year**, and two checks of the same kind must not be held **within four months of one another**.

### 5.93 Base and Line Checks

Under the ICAO and JAR operating arrangements, operators check proficiency at regular intervals by means of **base checks** and **line checks**. These checks are commonly coordinated with assessments used to maintain **type ratings** and **instrument ratings**.

## Theoretical Knowledge Examinations

### 5.94 Examination Requirements

Applicants for a JAA professional pilot licence or instrument rating demonstrate the required theoretical competence by passing approved examinations. The authority determines an acceptable examination language. Questions are presented in **multiple-choice format**, selected by computer from the **Central Question Bank (CQB)**.

### 5.95 Passing Standards and Examination Time Limits

The minimum passing score is **75%**. The complete group of examinations must be passed within **18 months**, measured from the end of the calendar month in which the first examination was attempted.

Earlier passes cease to count if the candidate:

- Fails the **same subject on the fourth attempt**.
- Does not pass all required subjects within **six sittings**.
- Does not complete all required subjects before the **18-month period** ends.

### 5.96 Period for Acceptance of Examination Passes

A complete set of theoretical passes remains acceptable for the issue of a **CPL(A) or IR(A)** for **36 months** from the date all required subjects were passed.

If the IR(A) is obtained during that **36-month** window, the ATPL(A) theory passes remain acceptable for **seven years from the final validity date of the IR(A) entered in the CPL(A)** when applying for the ATPL(A).
