# Chapter 28 — Addendum: EASA Part-FCL & Part-MED

## Chapter Five. Flight Crew Licensing

The material in this addendum is intended for study once the JAA learning objectives for aircrew licensing have been brought into alignment with EASA Part-FCL and EASA Part-MED.

## European Aviation Safety Agency (EASA)

### 5.1 Documentation

**EASA Part-FCL** is the EASA counterpart of JAR-FCL 1. It specifies the framework under which pilot licences, associated ratings and certificates are issued, together with the requirements governing their validity and the circumstances in which their privileges may be exercised.

**EASA Part-MED** corresponds to JAR-FCL 3. It establishes the medical certification requirements applicable to pilots and student pilots, including the issue of medical certificates, their validity periods, and the processes for revalidation and renewal.

### 5.2 Terminology and Abbreviations

**Aeroplane:** A powered, heavier-than-air aircraft with fixed wings that obtains its in-flight support from aerodynamic reactions acting on those wings.

**ATO — Approved Training Organization:** An organization approved to provide the applicable flight crew training.

**CAT — Commercial Air Transport:** The abbreviated designation for commercial air transport.

**Competent Authority:** The authority appointed by a Member State to handle applications for pilot licences and their associated ratings and certificates.

**Co-pilot:** A pilot other than the pilot-in-command who performs flight crew duties in an aircraft required to have more than one pilot. A person carried solely to receive instruction towards a licence or rating is not included in this definition.

**Cross-country:** A flight planned between separate departure and destination points along a predetermined route, flown using recognized navigation procedures.

**Dual Instruction Time:** Either airborne flight time or instrument ground time during which an appropriately authorized instructor provides instruction to the trainee.

**Full Flight Simulator (FFS):** A full-scale reproduction of the flight deck of a particular aircraft type or of a specified make, model and series. It includes the installed equipment and computing systems needed to simulate ground and airborne operation, an external-view visual system, and a motion system that provides force cues.

**Flight Time:** The period beginning when an aircraft initially moves with the intention of taking off and ending when it finally stops at the conclusion of that flight.

**FSTD — Flight Simulation Training Device:** A flight simulation training device which, as described here, does not necessarily include an external visual display or a force-cueing motion system.

**Instrument Flight Time:** Airborne time during which a pilot controls the aircraft using flight instruments alone.

**Instrument Ground Time:** Time spent receiving simulated instrument-flight instruction in an FSTD.

**IR — Instrument Rating:** The qualification denoted by this abbreviation.

**LAPL(A) — Light Aircraft Pilot Licence (Aeroplanes):** The light-aircraft licence applicable to aeroplanes.

**Night:** The interval beginning at the end of evening civil twilight and ending at the start of morning civil twilight. A Member State may instead specify another interval falling between sunset and sunrise through its appropriate authority.

**Pilot-in-Command (PIC):** The pilot assigned command responsibility, including responsibility for the flight's safe conduct.

**Pilot-in-Command Under Supervision (PICUS):** A co-pilot who carries out the responsibilities and functions of a PIC while remaining under the supervision of the designated PIC.

**Proficiency Check:** An assessment of practical competence required to renew or revalidate a rating; an oral examination may form part of the assessment.

**Renewal:** Administrative restoration of the privileges of an expired rating or certificate for a further defined period, following the applicable requirements.

**Revalidation:** Administrative continuation of the privileges of a rating or certificate while it is still valid, for a further specified period after the relevant conditions have been satisfied.

**Student Pilot-in-Command (SPIC):** A student acting in the PIC role while an instructor is aboard solely to observe. The instructor neither directs nor takes control of the flight.

**Touring Motor Glider (TMG):** A class of powered sailplane fitted with a permanently installed engine and propeller, neither of which retracts. Under the conditions of its flight manual it must be capable of independently taking off and climbing using its installed powerplant.

### 5.3 Application and Issue of Licences, Ratings and Certificates

Applications for the initial issue, revalidation or renewal of any licence, rating or certificate must be lodged with the competent authority. Applicants must use the submission method and documentation format that authority has approved.

An individual may hold no more than **one pilot licence for each aircraft category**.

### 5.4 Theoretical Knowledge Examinations

#### Responsibilities of the applicant

For a particular licence or rating, all required theoretical examination papers must be taken under the responsibility of a single Member State.

Before taking those papers, an applicant must have attended the appropriate approved course and must obtain a recommendation from an ATO. The ATO's recommendation remains effective for **12 months**. An applicant who does not attempt any paper during that period must undertake additional training to the extent determined by the ATO.

#### Pass standards

A mark of at least **75%** is required in each examination. Incorrect responses must not attract negative marks.

The examination series for a licence or rating must be completed successfully within **18 months**, measured from the end of the calendar month in which the first paper was attempted.

The applicant must repeat the entire examination series, after additional ATO training as determined by the organization, if any one of the following occurs:

- A particular paper has not been passed within **four attempts**.
- The complete examination series has not been passed within **six sittings**.
- The entire series has not been completed within the applicable **18-month** period.

#### Validity of theoretical knowledge passes

| Qualification | Period of validity |
|---|---|
| LAPL(A) and PPL(A) | **24 months** from successful completion of the examinations |
| CPL(A) and IR(A) | **36 months** from successful completion of the examinations |
| ATPL(A) | **7 years**, measured from the most recent validity date of an IR entered in the licence |

#### Practical skills test

The relevant theoretical examinations must be passed before an applicant undertakes the practical skills test for a licence, rating or certificate, except where the applicant is participating in an integrated flying course. Even in an integrated course, the required theoretical instruction must be finished before the practical test takes place.

### 5.5 Crediting of Flight Time and Theoretical Knowledge

#### Flight-time credit — PIC and instructional time

For the total flight-time requirement of a licence, rating or certificate, credit is awarded in full for solo flight, flight instruction received and flight as PIC.

On completion of an integrated ATPL course, up to **50 hours of student PIC instrument time** may contribute to the required PIC experience for an ATPL(A), CPL(A), or multi-engine class or type rating. A graduate of an integrated CPL/IR course may apply the corresponding credit towards the PIC requirements of a CPL(A) or multi-engine class or type rating.

#### Credit towards a higher licence

A pilot already licensed who performs duties as co-pilot or PICUS may count the entire qualifying co-pilot flight time towards the total experience requirement of a higher-grade licence.

#### Theoretical knowledge credit

- Passing the **ATPL(A)** theoretical examination series satisfies the theoretical examination requirement for an **LAPL(A), PPL(A) and CPL(A)**.
- Passing the **CPL(A)** theoretical examination series satisfies the corresponding requirement for an **LAPL(A) and PPL(A)**.
- Passing the **IR** theoretical examination provides credit for the theoretical examinations for an IR in another aircraft category.

### 5.6 Exercise of the Privileges of Licences

A licence holder may exercise the privileges granted by the licence only while the applicable ratings and medical certificate remain valid.

### 5.7 Obligation to Carry and Present Documents

Whenever exercising licence privileges, a pilot must have the following available:

- The pilot licence.
- The current medical certificate.
- An identification document bearing the pilot's photograph.

If an authorized representative or competent authority requests the pilot's flight-time record, the pilot must make it available without unnecessary delay.

### 5.8 Recording of Flight Time

Every pilot must maintain an accurate and dependable record of each flight, with the required particulars entered according to the procedures established by the competent authority.

### 5.9 Language Proficiency

EASA recognizes the following English-language proficiency levels:

| Level | Designation |
|---|---|
| 4 | Operational |
| 5 | Extended |
| 6 | Expert |

A pilot must hold at least **Level 4 (Operational)** in English, and the level achieved must be endorsed on the licence.

For a holder of **Level 4**, reassessment takes place every **4 years**. A **Level 5** endorsement requires reassessment every **6 years**. **Level 6** proficiency does not require periodic reassessment.

### 5.10 Recent Experience

#### Operation in CAT or while carrying passengers

A pilot acting as PIC or co-pilot must not operate in commercial air transport or carry passengers unless, during the **previous 90 days**, the pilot has completed at least **three take-offs, approaches and landings** in an aircraft of the same type or in an FFS representing the applicable type or class.

#### PIC at night

For operation as PIC at night, the pilot must satisfy either of the following:

- During the preceding **90 days**, perform at least **one take-off, approach and landing at night** as pilot flying, using an aircraft of the same type or an FFS representing the type or class; or
- Hold an **Instrument Rating**.

### 5.11 Licence Holders Aged 60 or More in Commercial Air Transport (CAT)

A pilot who has reached **60 years** but is **younger than 65 years** may participate as a pilot in CAT only in a **multi-crew** operation, and only when no other pilot in that crew has reached 60 years of age.

From **65 years of age**, a pilot may no longer serve as a pilot on an aircraft conducting CAT.

## Licences

### 5.12 Light Aircraft Pilot Licence (Aeroplanes) — LAPL(A)

The LAPL(A) provides a form of recreational pilot qualification with limitations on aircraft maximum take-off mass and passenger carriage. The required instruction must be completed at an ATO.

#### Privileges and conditions

The minimum age for issue is **17 years**. The holder may act as PIC of a single-engine piston aeroplane (land) or a TMG, provided that all of the following conditions are met:

- The aircraft has a **maximum take-off mass (MTOM) not exceeding 2,000 kg**.
- No more than **three passengers** are carried, so the total number of occupants never exceeds **four**.
- No remuneration is received for operating the flight.
- The flight is conducted as a **non-commercial operation**.
- Before carrying passengers for the first time after licence issue, the pilot has completed a further **10 hours as PIC** on aeroplanes or TMGs.

#### Experience requirements

The training programme must provide at least **30 hours of flight instruction** in aeroplanes or TMGs, including:

- **15 hours** or more of dual instruction.
- At least **6 hours** of supervised solo flight, of which **3 hours** or more are solo cross-country time.
- Within that solo cross-country experience, at least **one flight of 150 km (80 NM)** involving a full-stop landing at an aerodrome other than the aerodrome used for departure.

#### Recent experience

An LAPL(A) holder may exercise licence privileges only after meeting both of these requirements during the preceding **24 months**:

- At least **12 hours as PIC**, including **12 take-offs and 12 landings**.
- At least **1 hour** of refresher flying with an instructor.

### 5.13 Private Pilot Licence (Aeroplanes) — PPL(A)

#### Privileges and conditions

The minimum age for a PPL(A) is **17 years**.

This licence permits the holder to act, without payment, as either PIC or co-pilot on aeroplanes and TMGs conducting non-commercial flights.

An exception concerning remuneration applies to PPL(A) holders who also possess the necessary instructor or examiner qualification. They may be paid for providing LAPL(A) or PPL(A) instruction, carrying out the related skill tests and proficiency checks, and undertaking such checks for the associated ratings and certificates.

#### Theoretical knowledge

Applicants must pass examinations covering all nine subjects:

1. Air Law
2. Human Performance
3. Meteorology
4. Communications
5. Principles of Flight
6. Operational Procedures
7. Flight Performance and Planning
8. Aircraft General Knowledge
9. Navigation

#### Experience and crediting

A PPL(A) applicant must accumulate at least **45 hours of flight instruction** on aeroplanes. Up to **5 hours** may be completed in an FSTD. The experience must include:

- At least **25 hours** of dual instruction.
- At least **10 hours** of supervised solo flying, including **5 hours** or more of solo cross-country flight.
- At least one solo cross-country flight covering **270 km (150 NM)** or more, with full-stop landings at **two aerodromes** other than the point of departure.

#### Upgrade from LAPL(A) to PPL(A)

An LAPL(A) holder seeking a PPL(A) must first acquire **15 hours of flight time in aeroplanes after LAPL(A) issue**. At least **10 of those hours** must be part of an ATO training course.

Included in that course must be a minimum of **4 hours** of supervised solo flight, at least **2 hours** of which are solo cross-country time. This training must include a solo cross-country flight of **270 km (150 NM)** or more, with full-stop landings at **two different aerodromes** other than the departure aerodrome.

### 5.14 Commercial Pilot Licence (Aeroplanes) — CPL(A)

#### Privileges and conditions

The minimum age for a CPL(A) is **18 years**. Within the limits of the relevant operating requirements, the holder is entitled to:

1. Exercise the privileges of both the LAPL(A) and PPL(A).
2. Serve as PIC or co-pilot on an aeroplane engaged in operations other than CAT.
3. Serve as PIC of a **single-pilot aeroplane** used for CAT.
4. Serve as a co-pilot in CAT.

The candidate must meet the class or type rating requirements relevant to the aeroplane employed for the CPL(A) skills test.

#### Training course and skills test

The required theoretical and flight training must be delivered by an ATO. The applicant must then pass the appropriate practical skills test.

#### Theoretical knowledge

The prescribed examination subjects are:

1. Air Law
2. Aircraft General Knowledge — Airframes, Systems and Powerplant
3. Aircraft General Knowledge — Instrumentation
4. Mass and Balance
5. Performance
6. Flight Planning and Monitoring
7. Human Performance
8. Meteorology
9. General Navigation
10. Radio Navigation
11. Operational Procedures
12. Principles of Flight
13. VFR Communications

#### CPL(A) course and assessment

The applicant must complete the required programme of ground and flight training at an ATO and pass the skills test set out in **Appendix 4 of EASA Part-FCL**. The specific provisions for holders of an MPL apply where relevant.

#### Additional requirements for MPL holders

Before an MPL holder may use CPL(A) privileges, the following experience and training must have been completed in an aeroplane:

- **70 hours** of PIC-equivalent flying, consisting either entirely of PIC time or of at least **10 hours as PIC** with the balance accrued as PICUS.
- Within the 70-hour requirement, **20 hours** of VFR cross-country time, with at least **10 hours as PIC** and **10 hours as PICUS**. This includes a VFR PIC cross-country flight of at least **540 km (300 NM)** incorporating full-stop landings at **two separate aerodromes**.
- If the applicant already holds a valid IR, at least **15 hours** of dual visual-flight instruction at an ATO.
- At least **5 hours** of flight instruction at an ATO in an aeroplane capable of carrying **four or more persons**, fitted with a **variable-pitch propeller** and **retractable landing gear**.
- Successful completion of the **CPL(A) skills test**.

#### Forms of CPL(A) training at an ATO

Training may be arranged as a **CPL/IR integrated course**, a **CPL integrated course**, or a **CPL modular course**, all for aeroplanes.

#### CPL/IR integrated course — entry and credit

This programme accepts candidates entering with no prior flying qualification as well as PPL(A) or PPL(H) holders.

For candidates already holding a PPL(A) or PPL(H), **50%** of the flight hours completed before course entry may be recognized. That credit is limited to **40 hours**, or **45 hours** where an aeroplane night rating is already held. As many as **20 hours** of the recognized flying may satisfy the dual-instruction requirement.

#### CPL/IR integrated course — flight training

A total of **180 hours** is required, and no more than **40 hours** of this total may be instrument ground time. The programme must contain at least:

- **80 hours** of dual instruction, with no more than **40 hours** counted as instrument ground instruction.
- **70 hours as PIC**, encompassing VFR and instrument flying. Instrument flight recorded as SPIC can be credited as PIC time, but only to a limit of **20 hours**.
- **50 hours** of PIC cross-country flying. This includes a VFR flight of at least **540 km (300 NM)** with full-stop landings at **two aerodromes** other than the departure aerodrome.
- **5 hours** of night flying, including **3 hours of dual instruction**, at least **1 hour** of cross-country navigation and **five solo full-stop landings**.
- **100 hours** of instrument time, of which at least **20 hours** are flown as SPIC, and at least **50 hours** comprise instrument flight instruction.

For the instrument-flight-instruction component, the permitted simulated-instrument-ground credits are:

- Up to **25 hours** in an **FNPT I**; or
- Up to **40 hours** in an **FNPT II**, **FTD 2** or **FFS**. Within this second allowance, a maximum of **10 hours** may be undertaken in an FNPT I.

The course also requires **5 hours** in an aeroplane certified for at least **four occupants** and fitted with a variable-pitch propeller and retractable landing gear.

At the end of training, candidates must complete both the **CPL(A) skills test** and an **IR skills test** on either a single-engine or multi-engine aeroplane. They must also have completed an **approved multi-crew cooperation (MCC) course**.

#### CPL integrated and CPL modular courses

The separate provisions for these two aeroplane courses are contained in **Appendix 3 of EASA Part-FCL**.

### 5.15 Multi-Crew Pilot Licence — MPL

#### Outline

The MPL is based on competence developed progressively within a multi-crew operating environment. EASA Part-FCL specifies the fundamental programme, while the sponsoring airline operator, ATO and competent authority work together to design an integrated course suited to their collective requirements. The qualification represents a different approach to pilot preparation, and its use has increased since its introduction.

On finishing the programme, the candidate must pass a skills test in the aeroplane type used during the advanced training phase or in an FFS that reproduces that type.

#### Privileges and conditions

The licence may be issued from **18 years of age**. It authorizes the holder to act as **co-pilot** on an aeroplane that is required to operate with a co-pilot.

#### Theoretical knowledge

The MPL programme must include at least **750 hours** of instruction at ATPL(A) theoretical knowledge level, together with the instructional hours needed for the relevant type rating.

#### Flying training

At least **240 hours** of training must be undertaken as **pilot flying (PF)** or **pilot not flying (PNF)**, using actual and simulated flight. These hours are structured in four phases:

1. **Core flying skills:** Establishes fundamental single-pilot handling skills in an aeroplane.
2. **Basic:** Introduces instrument flight and the principles of multi-crew operation.
3. **Intermediate:** Applies multi-crew techniques to a high-performance, multi-engine turbine aeroplane.
4. **Advanced:** Provides type-rating instruction within an airline operating environment.

The airborne component must incorporate **upset-recovery training**, **night flying**, **flight entirely by reference to instruments**, the experience needed for the relevant type rating, and the experience necessary to develop the appropriate airmanship. The applicable **MCC** elements are integrated into these training phases.

### 5.16 Airline Transport Pilot Licence (Aeroplanes) — ATPL(A)

#### Privileges and conditions

The minimum age is **21 years**. An ATPL(A) holder may exercise the privileges of an LAPL(A), PPL(A) and CPL(A), and may serve as **PIC of an aeroplane operating in CAT**.

The applicant must meet the type-rating requirements for the aeroplane used during the ATPL(A) skills test.

#### Approved course options

Training must be completed at an ATO through either an **integrated** or a **modular** course.

#### Theoretical knowledge

The examination programme comprises:

1. Air Law
2. Aircraft General Knowledge — Airframes, Systems and Powerplant
3. Aircraft General Knowledge — Instrumentation
4. Mass and Balance
5. Performance
6. Flight Planning and Monitoring
7. Human Performance
8. Meteorology
9. General Navigation
10. Radio Navigation
11. Operational Procedures
12. Principles of Flight
13. VFR Communications
14. IFR Communications

#### Prerequisites

Before ATPL(A) issue, the applicant must hold either:

- An **MPL**; or
- A **CPL(A)** and a **multi-engine IR(A)**, together with instruction in **MCC**.

#### Experience requirements

At least **1,500 hours of flight time in aeroplanes** are required. Within this total, the candidate must meet all of the following conditions:

- **500 hours** in multi-pilot aeroplane operations.
- The required command experience, satisfied by one of these alternatives:
  - **500 hours as PICUS**; or
  - **250 hours as PIC**; or
  - **250 hours combined**, including **70 hours or more as PIC**, with the balance as PICUS.
- **200 hours** of cross-country flight, including at least **100 hours as PIC or PICUS**.
- **75 hours** of instrument time, of which no more than **30 hours** may be instrument ground time.
- **100 hours** of night flying as PIC or co-pilot.

Up to **100 hours** of the overall 1,500-hour total may be credited from training in an **FFS or FNPT**. Of this simulator allowance, no more than **25 hours** may come from an FNPT.

#### Crediting experience from other aircraft categories

A licence holder with flight experience in other aircraft categories may obtain credit up to these limits:

- **TMGs or sailplanes:** **30 hours** of PIC time.
- **Helicopters:** **50%** of the applicable flight-time requirements stated above.

#### Skills test

The applicant must demonstrate, through a skills test, the competence to operate a **multi-pilot aeroplane under IFR as PIC**.

## Ratings

### 5.17 Instrument Rating — Aeroplanes, IR(A)

#### General

An aeroplane may be flown under IFR by a PPL(A), CPL(A), MPL or ATPL(A) holder only if the pilot also has an IR appropriate to the aeroplane category. The stated exceptions apply to flights conducted for a skills test or as dual instruction.

#### Privileges

An IR(A) authorizes IFR aeroplane operation with a minimum **decision height of 200 ft (60 m)**. A holder of a **multi-engine IR(A)** may be authorized to use decision heights below **200 ft (60 m)** after appropriate specialist ATO instruction and successful completion of the required skills test.

#### Prerequisites and crediting

The applicant must hold at least one of the following qualifications:

- A **PPL(A) with a night rating**.
- An **ATPL(A) in another aeroplane category**, as expressed in the applicable training requirements.
- A **CPL** in the appropriate aeroplane category.

Additionally, the applicant must have flown at least **50 hours of cross-country as PIC**, with at least **10 hours** in the relevant aeroplane category.

#### Theoretical and flight instruction

An ATO must provide the necessary theoretical and practical training through an **integrated** or **modular** programme. The theoretical papers are:

1. Air Law
2. Aircraft General Knowledge — Instrumentation
3. Flight Performance and Monitoring
4. Human Performance
5. Meteorology
6. Radio Navigation
7. IFR Communications

Applicants must successfully complete the skills test described in **Appendix 7 of Part-FCL**.

#### Validity and revalidation

The IR(A) has a validity period of **1 year**. For continuation of privileges:

- Revalidation must take place during the **three months immediately before expiry**.
- A holder who fails the required proficiency check before expiry must not use IR privileges until the check has been passed.
- Following expiry, the candidate must undertake **refresher training at an ATO** before repeating the proficiency check.
- If the rating has neither been revalidated nor renewed during the preceding **7 years**, the applicant must retake both the **IR theoretical knowledge examination** and the **IR skills test**.
- An **FNPT II** or **FFS** may be used for an IR revalidation, but **every alternate proficiency check** must take place in an aeroplane.

### 5.18 Class and Type Ratings

#### When a rating is required

A pilot must hold an appropriate valid **class or type rating** before acting in any piloting role on an aircraft. Exceptions are made while the pilot is receiving flight instruction or undergoing a skills test or proficiency check.

#### Requirements for initial issue

Both ground and flight instruction must be completed at an ATO. The associated skills test must be passed no later than **six months after the course begins**, and must also fall within the **six months before the rating application**.

#### Rating validity and renewal

Ordinarily, class and type ratings are valid for **one year**. A **single-pilot, single-engine class rating** instead has a **two-year** validity period.

If a rating has already expired, the pilot must complete refresher training at an ATO before taking the renewal proficiency check.

#### Revalidation of multi-engine ratings

The proficiency check for a multi-engine class or type rating must be passed in the **three months immediately preceding expiry**. Where applicable, this assessment may also be used in conjunction with the proficiency check required to revalidate an IR(A).

#### Experience needed for initial class or type rating issue

For a **single-pilot multi-engine aeroplane**, at least **70 hours as PIC on aeroplanes** are required.

For a **multi-pilot aeroplane**, applicants must have all of the following:

- At least **70 hours of flight experience as PIC** on aeroplanes.
- A **multi-engine IR(A)**.
- A pass in the **ATPL(A) theoretical knowledge examinations**.
- Evidence of satisfactory completion of an aeroplane **MCC course**.

### 5.19 Night Rating — Aeroplanes

The aeroplane night rating enables a PPL(A) holder to fly under **VFR at night**.

Training at an ATO must provide the necessary theory and at least **5 hours of night flying** in the relevant aeroplane category. This must include:

- At least **3 hours** of dual instruction.
- At least **1 hour** of cross-country flight covering **50 km** or more.
- **Five solo take-offs** and **five solo full-stop landings**.

## Certificates

### 5.20 Instructor Certificate — Aeroplanes

#### General

A person conducting airborne flight instruction in an aeroplane must hold **both** a pilot licence and an instructor certificate appropriate to the instruction being provided.

An instructor certificate with the relevant instructional privileges is likewise required to deliver **synthetic flight instruction** or **MCC instruction**.

#### Eligibility and requirements

The minimum age for an instructor is **18 years**. An instructor must possess the licence, and where relevant the rating, associated with the instruction to be given.

The applicant must also:

- Have logged at least **15 hours** as pilot on the aircraft class or type to be used for instruction, including no more than **7 hours in an FSTD**; **or** have passed an assessment of competence for the appropriate instructor category on that class or type; and
- Be entitled to act as **PIC** on the aeroplane during instruction.

#### Training and assessment

Applicants must complete an ATO-delivered programme of theoretical and practical instruction complying with Part-FCL. They must then pass an **assessment of competence** establishing that they can instruct a student to the standard required for issue of the appropriate licence, rating or certificate.

An instructor certificate remains valid for **3 years**.

#### Additional Flight Instructor (FI) requirements

For instruction towards a **CPL**, an FI must have accumulated at least **500 hours** as a pilot in the relevant aircraft category, including no fewer than **200 hours of flight instruction**.

For **IR** instruction, an FI must meet the following requirements:

- At least **200 hours** of flight time under IFR, of which as many as **50 hours** may be instrument ground time.
- Completion of the IR training course as a student and a successful assessment of competence for an **Instrument Rating Instructor (IRI)** certificate.
- Where instruction is to be provided in multi-engine aeroplanes, satisfaction of the qualification requirements for a **Class Rating Instructor (CRI)**.

### 5.21 Examiner Certificate (Aeroplanes) — FE(A)

#### General

An examiner authorized to conduct a skills test, proficiency check or assessment of competence must:

- Hold the relevant equivalent licence, rating or certificate for the qualification being assessed and hold instructional privileges for it.
- Be authorized to act as **PIC** in the aeroplane while the test, check or assessment is conducted.
- Complete a suitable standardization course provided by an ATO or the competent authority.

An examiner certificate is valid for **3 years**.

#### Privileges and conditions

Subject to the corresponding experience minima, an FE(A) may conduct the following assessments:

| Assessment authority | Minimum experience as aeroplane or TMG pilot | Of which flight instruction |
|---|---:|---:|
| PPL(A) initial skills tests, and skill tests/proficiency checks for associated single-pilot class or type ratings other than high-performance complex aeroplanes | **1,000 hours** | **250 hours** |
| CPL(A) initial skills tests, and skill tests/proficiency checks for associated single-pilot class or type ratings other than high-performance complex aeroplanes | **2,000 hours** | **250 hours** |
| LAPL(A) skills tests and proficiency checks | **500 hours** | **100 hours** |

## EASA Part-MED

### 5.22 Abbreviations and Definitions

#### Abbreviations

| Abbreviation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **AeMC** | Aero-medical centre |
| **AME** | Aero-medical examiner |
| **CAT** | Commercial air transport |
| **GMP** | General medical practitioner |
| **OHMP** | Occupational health medical practitioner |
| **OML** | Operational multi-pilot limitation |
| **OPL** | Operational passenger limitation |
| **OSL** | Operational safety-pilot limitation |

#### Accredited medical conclusion

A medical judgment reached by one or more medical experts acceptable to the licensing authority, using objective and non-discriminatory criteria relevant to the case. Where necessary, the decision is informed by consultation with flight-operations specialists or other experts.

#### Assessment

A determination of an individual's medical fitness based on examination of the person's medical history and the aero-medical examinations required under Part-MED. Additional medical investigations may also be considered, including an ECG, measurement of blood pressure, blood tests, X-rays or other clinically necessary procedures.

#### Colour safe

The ability to distinguish without difficulty the colours used for air navigation and to identify coloured aviation lights correctly.

#### Limitation

An enforceable condition recorded on a medical certificate, pilot licence or cabin crew medical report. The holder must comply with the condition whenever exercising the privileges covered by the relevant licence or attestation.

#### Medical certificates applicable to pilot licences

| Licence or applicant | Minimum medical certification requirement |
|---|---|
| Student pilot | Medical certificate appropriate to the intended licence before any solo flight |
| LAPL | LAPL medical certificate |
| PPL | Class 2 medical certificate |
| CPL | Class 1 medical certificate |
| MPL | Class 1 medical certificate |
| ATPL | Class 1 medical certificate |

Two further requirements apply:

- When a **night rating** is added to a PPL or LAPL, the pilot must demonstrate that they are **colour safe**.
- A PPL holder adding an **IR** must undergo **audiometry** at the frequency and to the standards prescribed for **Class 1** medical certification.

#### Decrease in medical fitness

A licence holder must not use licence, rating or certificate privileges when any of these circumstances could compromise the safe performance of pilot duties:

- An identified deterioration in medical fitness.
- Use of a prescribed or non-prescribed medication likely to affect safe performance.
- Medical, surgical or other treatment that may interfere with flight safety.

In addition, aero-medical advice must be obtained **without undue delay** following any of these events:

- Surgery or another invasive procedure.
- Beginning regular medication.
- A significant personal injury that prevents the person from carrying out flight crew duties.
- A significant illness with a similar effect on flight crew fitness.
- Pregnancy.
- Admission to a hospital or medical clinic.
- A first requirement for vision-correcting lenses.

For **Class 1 or Class 2** medical certificate holders, the assessment must be sought from an **AeMC or AME**. That examiner or medical centre assesses fitness and determines whether the pilot may resume exercising licence privileges.

For **LAPL** medical certificate holders, the assessment may be obtained from an **AeMC**, **AME**, or the **GMP who signed the certificate**. The practitioner or centre evaluates fitness and decides whether the licence privileges may be resumed.

#### Initial issue of medical certificates

| Certificate | Authorized initial issuer |
|---|---|
| Class 1 | **AeMC** |
| Class 2 | **AeMC or AME** |
| LAPL | **AeMC or AME**, or a **GMP** where national law permits |

#### Revalidation and renewal of medical certificates

Both Class 1 and Class 2 medical certificates may be revalidated or renewed by an **AeMC or AME**. A LAPL medical certificate may be revalidated or renewed by an **AeMC or AME** or, where national legislation allows, by a **GMP**.

#### Medical certificate validity

| Medical certificate | Holder's age or operational situation | Validity |
|---|---|---|
| **Class 1** | Standard validity | **12 months** |
| **Class 1** | Single-pilot CAT passenger operation from **age 40**, or any holder who has reached **age 60** | **6 months** |
| **Class 2** | Before reaching **age 40** | **60 months**; a certificate issued before 40 cannot extend beyond the holder's **42nd birthday** |
| **Class 2** | Between **40 and 50 years** | **24 months**; one issued before the 50th birthday cannot extend beyond the holder's **51st birthday** |
| **Class 2** | After reaching **age 50** | **12 months** |
| **LAPL** | Before reaching **age 40** | **60 months**; a certificate issued before 40 cannot extend beyond the holder's **42nd birthday** |
| **LAPL** | After reaching **age 40** | **24 months** |

The start date used in calculating these validity periods is the **date of the medical examination**.

#### Revalidation

The medical examination or assessment for revalidation may be carried out in the **45 days preceding certificate expiry**. If the required revalidation arrangements are not completed, the holder must instead follow the renewal examination or assessment procedure.

#### Renewal

For a **Class 1 or Class 2** medical certificate that has been expired for **more than 2 years**, an AeMC or AME must first assess the applicant's aero-medical records before carrying out the renewal examination.

Where the medical certificate has been expired for **more than 5 years**, the medical assessment must meet the **initial-issue examination requirements**.

#### Operational limitation codes

**Operational Multi-Pilot Limitation (OML) — Class 1 only**

Where a CPL, MPL or ATPL holder does not satisfy all Class 1 medical standards and is referred to the licensing authority, that authority evaluates whether an OML is suitable. The limitation specifies that the licence is **valid only as, or with, a qualified co-pilot**.

A pilot subject to OML may fly only in multi-pilot operations accompanied by a qualified co-pilot who **has no OML restriction** and is **younger than 60 years**. Only the licensing authority has the power to impose or remove this limitation.

**Operational Safety-Pilot Limitation (OSL) — Class 2 and LAPL**

An OSL holder may operate an aircraft only with an additional pilot on board who is qualified to act as PIC, occupies a seat at the controls, and has access to **dual controls** fitted to the aircraft.

For **Class 2** medical certification, an AeMC or AME may impose this limitation after consultation with the licensing authority.

**Operational Passenger Limitation (OPL) — Class 2 and LAPL**

A licence holder whose certificate carries an OPL is permitted to operate the aircraft **only without passengers**.
