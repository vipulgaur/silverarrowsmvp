# Chapter 1: Definitions

## Introduction

The learning scope corresponds to **Air Law (Subject 010)** and its flight crew licensing learning objectives. Principal international foundations are the **Chicago Convention**, its annexes and procedures for air navigation, supplemented by **Part-FCL** and **Part-MED**.

## Air Law

Air Law brings together principles of airmanship and the international arrangements used to regulate and administer civil aviation. It encompasses international agreements, flight-crew licensing, rules of the air, instrument flight procedures, air traffic control, and aerodrome characteristics. Much of it relies on familiar operational practices, particularly for pilots holding a PPL. The syllabus and learning objectives determine the required knowledge and examination standard.

## Definitions

### A

**Advisory Airspace:** A defined volume of airspace, or a specified route, where an air traffic advisory service is available.

**Advisory Route:** A designated route on which aircraft may receive air traffic advisory service.

**Aerial Work Aircraft:** An aircraft used, or planned by its operator to be used, for aerial work rather than public transport.

**Aerial Work Undertaking:** An organization or business whose activities include carrying out aerial work.

**Aerobatic Manoeuvres:** Flight manoeuvres such as loops, spins, rolls, bunts, stall turns, inverted flight, and comparable aerobatic actions.

**Aerodrome (extended description):** Land or water prepared, equipped, reserved, or routinely used for aircraft arrivals and departures. This includes spaces at ground level, on rooftops, or elsewhere suited to aircraft that take off or land vertically. A location permanently abandoned for this purpose, with no return to aerodrome use, is excluded.

**Aerodrome Control Service:** The ATC service for aircraft on the aerodrome's manoeuvring area or apron, and for aircraft flying in or close to its aerodrome traffic zone while maintaining visual reference to the ground.

**Aerodrome Flight Information Unit:** A person designated by the relevant authority or aerodrome operator to pass information by radio to aircraft operating, or intending to operate, within the aerodrome traffic zone; the corresponding information provision constitutes aerodrome flight information service.

**Aerodrome (standard description):** A specified land or water area, together with its buildings, installations, and equipment, intended wholly or partly for aircraft arrival, departure, and surface movement.

**Aerodrome Control Tower:** An ATC unit responsible for providing aerodrome control to aerodrome traffic.

**Aerodrome Operating Minima:** The least acceptable operating conditions at a given aerodrome for a particular aircraft. The parameters encompass cloud ceiling and runway visual range for departure, and decision height or minimum descent height, runway visual range, and required visual cues for landing.

**Aerodrome Traffic:** Aircraft operating in the vicinity of an aerodrome and all traffic using its manoeuvring area.

**Aerodrome Traffic Zone (ATZ):** A defined portion of airspace surrounding an aerodrome, established to protect its traffic.

**Aeronautical Ground Light:** A light installed to assist aerial navigation, excluding lighting carried on an aircraft.

**Aeronautical Part:** The airside portion of an aerodrome, including buildings where entry is controlled through security measures.

**Aeronautical Radio Station:** A surface-based radio installation that sends or receives signals to support aircraft.

**Aeronautical Station:** A ground station belonging to the aeronautical mobile service; in some cases the station may instead be located on a vessel or offshore platform.

**Aeroplane:** A powered, heavier-than-air aircraft whose lift is mainly generated by aerodynamic effects on surfaces that remain fixed in normal flight conditions.

**Aircraft:** A machine supported in the atmosphere by air reactions, excluding the reaction of air against the Earth's surface.

**Aircraft Category:** A grouping based on essential aircraft characteristics; examples include aeroplane, helicopter, glider, and free balloon.

**Aircraft Certified for Single-pilot Operation:** An aircraft type which its State of Registry has determined during certification can safely be operated with only one pilot.

**Aircraft Type:** Aircraft sharing a common basic design, including modifications that do not materially change handling or flying characteristics.

**Air Traffic:** Aircraft airborne or operating within the manoeuvring area of an aerodrome.

**Air Traffic Advisory Service:** A service offered in advisory airspace to help maintain separation, so far as practicable, between aircraft following IFR flight plans.

**Air Traffic Control Clearance:** ATC's permission for an aircraft to proceed subject to stated conditions. It may be called simply a *clearance*, or identified by its phase, such as taxi, take-off, departure, en-route, approach, or landing clearance.

**Air Traffic Control Service:** A service aimed at preventing collisions between aircraft and, on manoeuvring areas, between aircraft and obstructions; it also helps traffic move safely, efficiently, and in an orderly sequence.

**Air Traffic Control Unit:** A collective term for an area control centre, an approach control office, or an aerodrome control tower.

**Air Traffic Services Airspaces:** Delimited airspaces assigned alphabetic classes, each specifying the permitted flight operations, available ATS, and applicable operating rules.

**Air Traffic Services Reporting Office:** A unit which accepts pre-departure flight plans and reports connected with air traffic services.

**Air Traffic Services Unit:** Any ATC unit, flight information centre, or ATS reporting office.

**Air Transport Undertaking:** A business conducting flights to transport passengers or freight as public transport.

**Airborne Collision Avoidance System (ACAS):** An onboard system using signals from SSR transponders to warn pilots of potentially conflicting transponder-equipped aircraft. Its operation does not depend on ground-based systems.

**Airway:** A corridor-shaped control area, or part of one, supported by radio navigation aids.

**Alerting Service:** A service that informs the relevant organizations when aircraft require search-and-rescue assistance and helps those organizations when needed.

**Alternate Aerodrome:** A location to which an aircraft can proceed when continuing to, or landing at, its intended destination is not possible or advisable. Categories are:

- **Take-off alternate:** An aerodrome available for a landing soon after departure when the departure aerodrome cannot be used.
- **En-route alternate:** An aerodrome at which landing is possible following an abnormal condition or emergency during the journey.
- **Destination alternate:** An aerodrome that can be used if landing at the intended destination becomes impractical or inadvisable.

The departure aerodrome can also qualify as an en-route or destination alternate.

**Altitude:** A vertical measurement above mean sea level (MSL), whether of a point, object, or level.

**Annual Costs:** The most reasonable estimate, made at the time of a flight, of an aircraft's upkeep and indirect operating expenses for the calendar year beginning on the preceding 1 January. Direct costs are omitted, and the costs counted must be genuinely necessary and incurred without a profit objective.

**Annual Flying Hours:** The most reasonable estimate at the time of a flight of the aircraft's flying hours already completed or expected during the year beginning on the preceding 1 January.

**Approach Control Office:** An ATC unit serving controlled arrivals and departures at one or several aerodromes.

**Approach Control Service:** ATC provided to arriving or departing controlled flights.

**Approach to Landing:** The stage of an approach during which the aircraft descends below a height 1,000 ft above the specified decision height or minimum descent height.

**Appropriate ATS Authority:** The authority appointed by the State responsible for ATS in the airspace involved.

**Appropriate Authority:** Over the high seas, the relevant authority of the aircraft's State of Registry; elsewhere, the relevant authority of the State that has sovereignty over the territory beneath the flight.

**Apron:** A designated area of a land aerodrome used for loading and unloading people, post, or freight, as well as refuelling, parking, or maintaining aircraft.

**Area Control Centre (ACC):** An ATC unit providing area control to aircraft in a notified flight information region that are not receiving aerodrome or approach control.

**Area Control Service:** ATC supplied to controlled flights within control areas.

**Area Navigation Equipment (RNAV):** Airborne navigation equipment permitting flight on chosen routes using suitable ground aids, the capabilities of onboard navigation equipment, or both together.

**ATS Route:** A specified route for directing traffic flows to enable ATS provision. The expression includes airways, advisory routes, controlled and uncontrolled routes, and departure or arrival routes.

**Authorized Person:** A constable, someone authorized by the Secretary of State, or someone authorized by the relevant Authority. Authorization may be individual or by group/description and may be general or case-specific.

### C

**Cabin Attendant:** A person aboard a public-transport aircraft to perform passenger-safety duties assigned by the operator or aircraft commander, without serving as flight crew.

**Captive Flight:** Flight by an uncontrollable balloon that remains tethered to the surface.

**Cargo:** Freight which also includes mail and live animals.

**Certified for Single-pilot Operation:** An aircraft whose required minimum operating crew does not exceed one pilot.

**Change-over Point:** On an ATS route segment defined by VOR stations, the point where the principal navigation reference changes from the VOR behind the aircraft to the next VOR ahead. These points are normally at intervals of approximately 60 NM, chosen to balance usable signal strength and quality and to give aircraft common azimuth guidance along the route.

**Clearance Limit:** The furthest point to which an ATC clearance permits an aircraft to proceed.

**Cloud Ceiling:** At an aerodrome, the height above aerodrome elevation of the bottom of the lowest cloud layer visible from the aerodrome that hides more than half the sky.

**Commander:** The flight-crew member nominated as aircraft commander by the operator; if nobody has been nominated, the pilot currently acting as PIC.

**Commercial Pilot Licence (CPL):** A professional-pilot licence permitting its holder to exercise PPL privileges, act as PIC outside commercial air transport, command a single-pilot aeroplane in commercial air transport, and serve as co-pilot in commercial air transport requiring two pilots.

**Competent Authority:** The authority given responsibility by a State's law for advancing civil aviation safety.

**Contracting State:** A State bound as a party to the Convention on International Civil Aviation signed in Chicago on **7 December 1944**.

**Control Area (CTA):** Controlled airspace beginning at a prescribed level above the Earth's surface and extending upwards.

**Controlled Aerodrome:** An aerodrome with ATC service for its traffic. It need not be surrounded by a control zone.

**Controlled Airspace:** Defined airspace in which ATC is offered to IFR flights and, according to the applicable classification, VFR flights. The term encompasses ATS Classes **A, B, C, D, and E**.

**Controlled Flight:** A flight required to operate under an ATC clearance.

**Control Zone (CTR):** Controlled airspace starting at ground level and extending to a stated upper boundary.

**Configuration (Aeroplane):** The combined positions of movable parts, including flaps and undercarriage, which change an aeroplane's aerodynamic characteristics.

**Co-pilot:** A licensed pilot performing piloting duties other than those of PIC; a pilot aboard solely for flight instruction is not included.

**CPL (Current Flight Plan):** A flight plan updated to incorporate any subsequent changes arising from ATC clearances.

**Crew:** Flight-crew members, appointed persons on the flight deck who instruct, supervise, or assess flight crew, and cabin attendants.

**Critical Power-unit(s):** The engine or power units whose failure produces the worst effect on aircraft characteristics for the situation being considered.

**Cross-country Flight:** A flight from one point to another along a planned route using standard navigation procedures.

**Cruise Climb:** A technique in which an aeroplane gradually gains altitude in cruise as fuel consumption reduces its mass.

**Cruising Level:** The level occupied for a substantial portion of a flight.

### D–E

**Danger Area:** Delimited airspace in which activities hazardous to aircraft may take place at notified times.

**Day:** Under this definition, the interval beginning **30 minutes before sunrise** and ending **30 minutes after sunset**, with both endpoints excluded; sunrise and sunset are determined at surface level.

**Decision Height (DH):** During a precision approach, the height at which the pilot must begin a missed approach unless the required visual reference for continuing has been obtained.

**Design Landing Mass:** The greatest aircraft mass assumed for structural design when a landing is planned.

**Design Take-off Mass:** The maximum structural-design mass assumed at the beginning of the take-off run.

**Design Taxiing Mass:** The maximum mass assumed when designing the aircraft to withstand loads during ground operations before take-off.

**Destination Alternate:** An aerodrome designated as an alternative destination if landing at the intended destination becomes impossible or inadvisable.

**Dual Instruction Time:** Flight time spent receiving onboard instruction from a pilot qualified to instruct.

**En-route Clearance:** A later ATC clearance to the intended destination, following an initial clearance covering only the first segment of the flight to expedite departures.

**Essential Local Traffic:** Aircraft, people, or vehicles near the runway, and traffic in approach, departure, or climb-out areas, capable of creating a collision threat to arriving or departing flights.

**Estimated Off-blocks Time (EOBT):** The predicted time at which an aircraft will first move in connection with its departure.

**Estimated Time of Arrival (ETA):** For an IFR flight, the expected arrival time over the navigation-defined point at which the instrument approach will begin; where no associated navigation aid exists, the expected time over the aerodrome.

**Expected Approach Time (EAT):** The time ATC anticipates releasing a delayed arrival from the holding point to make its approach. The aircraft actually leaves the hold according to the subsequent approach clearance.

### F–H

**Final Approach and Take-off Area (FATO — Helicopters):** A designated area where a helicopter completes the final part of an approach to hover or landing and begins its take-off manoeuvre. For performance Class 1 helicopters, the available rejected-take-off area is also included.

**Filed Flight Plan (FPL):** The flight-plan details originally lodged with an ATS unit by a pilot or authorized representative, before any later amendments. When referred to as an FPL *message*, the term concerns the formatted flight-plan data transmitted.

**Flight Crew Member:** A licensed member of the crew carrying out duties necessary for the aircraft's operation during flight time.

**Flight Information Service (FIS):** Information and advice supplied to improve the safety and efficiency of flights.

**Flight Level (FL):** A constant-pressure surface referenced to **1013.2 hPa**, separated from other flight levels by specified pressure intervals. With a suitably calibrated pressure altimeter, QNH gives an indication of altitude, QFE gives height above the QFE datum, and the 1013.2 hPa setting provides flight-level indications. These height and altitude indications are pressure-derived (*altimetric*) rather than geometric measurements.

**Flight Plan:** Prescribed information made available to ATS about a proposed flight or a portion of it.

**Flight Procedures Trainer:** Refer to *Synthetic Flight Trainer*.

**Flight Simulator:** Refer to *Synthetic Flight Trainer*.

**Flight Recording System:** Equipment comprising a flight data recorder, a cockpit voice recorder, or both.

**Flight Time — Aeroplanes:** The period from the aircraft's first movement for the purpose of departing until it comes to its final stop after the flight. This corresponds to commonly used *block-to-block* or *chock-to-chock* time, from departure from the loading position to arrival at the unloading position. Under the accompanying helicopter note, time for which helicopter rotors are engaged counts towards flight time.

**Flight Time as Student Pilot-in-command:** Flight time during which the student performs PIC duties and the instructor watches without controlling or influencing the flight.

**Flight Visibility:** The distance the pilot can see forward from the cockpit while airborne.

**Free Balloon:** A balloon in flight that is not restrained by an attachment to the surface.

**Free Controlled Flight:** Untethered balloon flight during which balloon altitude can be controlled by the commander using an attached device or remotely. A tether of no more than **5 m**, used as part of the take-off process, is permitted within this definition.

**Ground Visibility:** Visibility reported for an aerodrome by an authorized observer.

**Government Aerodrome:** A UK aerodrome occupied by a government department or visiting military force.

**Heading:** The direction in which an aircraft's longitudinal axis points, measured in degrees from a specified north reference such as true, magnetic, compass, or grid north.

**Heavier-than-air Aircraft:** An aircraft receiving most of its flying lift from aerodynamic forces.

**Height:** Vertical distance between a point, object, or level and a specified reference datum.

### I–L

**IFR:** The abbreviation designating *Instrument Flight Rules*.

**IFR Flight:** A flight operated according to instrument flight rules.

**IMC:** The abbreviation designating *Instrument Meteorological Conditions*.

**Instrument Approach Procedure:** A protected sequence of instrument-guided manoeuvres extending from an initial approach fix, or the start of a defined arrival route, to a position from which landing can be completed; where landing does not occur, it extends to a point where holding or en-route obstacle-clearance criteria become applicable.

**Instrument Flight Time:** Time during which a pilot controls an aircraft using flight instruments alone, without relying on external visual references.

**Instrument Ground Time:** Time used to practise simulated instrument flight on the ground in a synthetic training device approved by the licensing authority.

**Instrument Meteorological Conditions (IMC):** Weather in which visibility, separation from cloud, or ceiling falls below the requirements for visual meteorological conditions. Within a control zone, ATC may authorize a VFR flight to continue in IMC.

**Instrument Time:** The combined category covering instrument flight time and instrument ground time.

**Joint Aviation Authorities (JAA):** An organization associated with the European Civil Aviation Conference.

**Joint Aviation Requirement (JAR):** A numbered requirement issued by the JAA under the applicable technical harmonization arrangements.

**Landing Area:** The portion of an aerodrome's movement area used for aircraft landing or take-off.

**Landing Surface:** A section of the aerodrome's land or water surface which the aerodrome authority declares suitable for the normal landing run in a particular direction.

**Level:** A general expression for an aircraft's vertical position, which may be given as height, altitude, or flight level.

**Lifejacket:** Equipment intended to keep an individual supported in or on water.

**Log Book:** An aircraft, engine, variable-pitch propeller, or personal flying record maintained in a physical book or in another form specifically accepted by the authority.

### M–N

**Manoeuvring Area:** Parts of an aerodrome allocated to aircraft take-off, landing, and taxiing, **excluding aprons**.

**Maintenance:** Work needed to preserve airworthiness, potentially including overhaul, inspection, repairs, replacement, modifications, and correction of defects, separately or together.

**Medical Assessment:** Evidence issued by a Contracting State confirming that an aircrew licence holder meets the prescribed medical standards. It is based on the licensing authority's evaluation of the designated medical examiner's report.

**Minimum Descent Height (MDH):** On a non-precision approach, the height below which further descent is not permitted until the required visual reference exists.

**Multiple-pilot Aeroplanes:** Aeroplanes certified to be operated by a crew of **at least two pilots**.

**Multi-crew Co-operation (MCC):** Flight crew members carrying out their tasks collaboratively under the leadership of the pilot-in-command.

**Movement Area:** The portions of an aerodrome for aircraft take-off, landing, and taxiing, including both the manoeuvring area and the apron or aprons.

**Nautical Mile (NM):** The international nautical mile, equal to **1,852 m**.

**Night:** The period from the end of evening civil twilight to the beginning of morning civil twilight, or another sunset-to-sunrise interval prescribed by the appropriate authority. Civil twilight ends or begins when the Sun's centre is **6° below the horizon**.

**Non-precision Approach:** An instrument approach using nonvisual guidance for azimuth or elevation but not meeting the definition of a precision approach.

**Normal Residency:** A place where a person lives for at least **185 days of each calendar year**, on the basis of occupational and personal ties, or—where no occupational ties exist—personal ties demonstrating a close connection with that location.

### P–R

**Private Pilot Licence (PPL):** A pilot licence that does not permit the holder to pilot an aircraft in exchange for remuneration under this definition.

**Pilot (verb):** To operate an aircraft's flight controls during flight time.

**Pilot-in-command (PIC):** The pilot with responsibility for the aircraft's conduct and safety throughout flight time.

**Pilot-in-command under Supervision (PICUS):** A co-pilot carrying out the functions and duties of PIC while under supervision of the designated pilot-in-command.

**Power Unit:** One or more engines together with their required ancillary equipment, capable of independently providing thrust without depending on any other power unit's continued operation; short-duration thrust-producing devices are not included.

**Precision Approach:** An instrument approach in which both lateral (azimuth) and vertical (elevation) guidance are provided by ILS, MLS, or precision approach radar.

**Pressure Altitude:** The altitude corresponding to a given atmospheric pressure under the Standard Atmosphere model.

**Proficiency Check:** A demonstration of competence used to renew or revalidate a rating, including any oral assessment requested by the examiner.

**Prohibited Area:** Defined airspace above a State's territory or territorial waters in which aircraft flight is forbidden.

**Rating:** A licence entry or associated authorization describing particular operating privileges, conditions, or restrictions.

**Renewal:** An administrative procedure carried out once a rating has expired.

**Rendering a Licence Valid:** A Contracting State's acceptance of a licence issued by another State as equivalent to its own, instead of issuing a fresh national licence.

**Repetitive Flight Plan (RPL):** A flight plan for a series of routinely repeated flights sharing the same basic features, submitted by an operator for ATS units to keep and apply repeatedly.

**Reporting Point:** A designated geographic location used as a reference for reporting an aircraft's position.

**Residency — Normal:** A residence occupied for **at least 185 days per calendar year** because of employment and personal connections, or because of close personal connections when no employment ties exist.

**Restricted Area:** Delimited airspace over land or territorial waters of a State in which aircraft operations are allowed only under prescribed conditions.

**Revalidation:** Administrative extension of a still-valid rating or approval after the holder completes the specified requirements, permitting continued exercise of its privileges for another prescribed period.

**Runway:** A defined rectangular part of a land aerodrome prepared for aircraft departures and landings.

**Runway Visual Range (RVR):** The distance along a runway in the take-off or landing direction over which runway lighting or surface markings are visible from the touchdown zone. It may be established by visual observation or instrumentation near that zone, or near the runway midpoint if touchdown-zone measurement is impracticable; it also includes the value communicated to the aircraft commander on behalf of the aerodrome authority.

### S

**Scheduled Journey:** One flight from a recurring sequence operated between the same two locations as a systematic service.

**Signal Area:** A designated aerodrome area where ground signals are displayed.

**Skills Test:** An assessment of practical competence for first issue of a licence or rating, including any oral questions the examiner considers necessary.

**Solo Flight Time:** Time logged while a student pilot is the only person on board an aircraft.

**Special VFR Flight (SVFR):** A visual-flight-rules flight operating in a control zone with ATC permission despite conditions being below the normal VMC minima.

**State of Design:** The State with regulatory jurisdiction over the organization responsible for an aircraft type's design.

**State of Registry:** The State in whose aircraft register the aircraft is recorded.

**Strayed Aircraft:** An aircraft that has moved a substantial distance from its intended track or has reported being lost.

**Student Pilot-in-command (SPIC):** A student acting as PIC while an instructor remains aboard solely to observe, without influencing or taking control of the flight.

**Synthetic Flight Trainer:** Ground equipment that simulates flight conditions, in any of three forms:

- **Flight simulator:** A detailed reproduction of a particular aircraft type's flight deck, realistically simulating cockpit mechanisms and systems, the flight-crew working environment, performance, and flying qualities.
- **Flight procedures trainer:** A credible cockpit environment simulating instrumentation, straightforward mechanical/electrical/electronic control functions, and the performance and handling of an aircraft class.
- **Basic instrument flight trainer:** Equipment with appropriate instruments that recreates the cockpit environment of flight under instrument conditions.

### T–V

**Take-off Surface:** A section of the aerodrome's land or water area officially made available for aircraft to perform a normal take-off run in a particular direction.

**Taxiing:** Aircraft movement across an aerodrome surface using its own power, excluding the take-off and landing phases.

**Taxiway:** A designated path on a land aerodrome connecting areas used by aircraft while taxiing. It may take these forms:

- **Aircraft stand taxilane:** A part of an apron designated solely to give aircraft access to stands.
- **Apron taxiway:** A taxiway section across an apron providing a through route.
- **Rapid-exit taxiway:** A runway exit joining at an acute angle, allowing landing aeroplanes to vacate at relatively high speed and shorten runway occupancy.

**Terminal Control Area (TCA):** A control area normally situated where ATS routes meet near one or more important aerodromes.

**Total Estimated Elapsed Time (EET):** For IFR flights, the predicted duration from take-off to the point, defined using navigational aids, where an instrument approach is intended to begin; if the destination has no associated navigation aid, the duration to arrival overhead the aerodrome. For VFR flights, the estimated time from take-off to arrival overhead the destination aerodrome.

**Track:** The course traced by an aircraft's movement projected onto the Earth's surface, normally stated as an angular direction from true, magnetic, or grid north.

**Traffic Avoidance Service:** Manoeuvring advice issued by an ATS unit to help a pilot avoid a collision.

**Traffic Information:** Reports from an ATS unit about observed or otherwise known traffic near the aircraft's position or intended route, helping the pilot recognize and avoid collision risk.

**Transition Altitude:** The altitude at or below which aircraft vertical position is expressed and controlled using altitude values rather than flight levels.

**Unidentified Aircraft:** An aircraft known to an ATC unit but whose identity has not been established.

**VFR:** The abbreviation indicating *Visual Flight Rules*.

**VFR Flight:** A flight carried out under visual flight rules.

**Visibility:** The distance at which atmospheric conditions allow a person to see and identify prominent unlit features in daylight, or prominent illuminated features during darkness.

**Visual Meteorological Conditions (VMC):** Conditions in which visibility, cloud separation, and ceiling are no worse than the prescribed visual-flight minima.

**VMC:** The abbreviation for *Visual Meteorological Conditions*.
