# Chapter 26 — Aircraft Accident and Incident Investigation

## Introduction

### 26.1 Annex 13

**ICAO Annex 13** establishes the Standards and Recommended Practices (SARPs) governing the investigation of aircraft accidents and incidents.

### 26.2 Applicability

Except where a particular provision establishes a different scope, the investigative requirements apply to action taken after an aircraft accident or incident, irrespective of where the event occurred.

The provisions of Annex 13 referring to the **State of the Operator** have a more specific application. They apply when an aircraft is operated under a lease, charter or interchange arrangement, the State of the Operator is different from the **State of Registry**, and the former State performs some or all of the latter's Annex 13 responsibilities and functions.

### 26.3 Accident

For the purposes of accident investigation, an **accident** is an occurrence connected with aircraft operation during the period beginning when a person boards the aircraft intending to fly and ending after every such person has disembarked. The occurrence is classified as an accident when one or more of the following circumstances applies.

**Fatal or serious injury to a person**

A person dies or sustains a serious injury through any of these circumstances:

- Their presence aboard the aircraft.
- Physical contact with the aircraft or any of its components, including components that have separated from it.
- Direct exposure to an aircraft's jet blast.

This injury criterion excludes injuries attributable to natural causes, those intentionally inflicted by the injured person or by somebody else, and injuries to stowaways concealed in areas not normally accessible to passengers or crew members.

**Significant aircraft damage or structural failure**

The aircraft suffers damage or a structural failure that satisfies both conditions below:

- Its structural integrity, operating performance or flight characteristics are adversely affected.
- Repair would normally involve major work or replacement of the damaged component.

The following damage does not, by itself, fall within this criterion: engine failure or damage confined to the engine, its cowling or accessories; damage confined to propellers, wingtips, antennas, tyres, brakes or fairings; and minor dents or punctures in the aircraft skin.

**Aircraft missing or inaccessible**

An occurrence also meets the accident definition if the aircraft cannot be located or is entirely inaccessible.

The distinction between an **accident** and a **serious incident** is based on the outcome of the occurrence, rather than a fundamentally different type of circumstance.

### Definitions

The following additional terms have specific meanings in aircraft accident and incident investigation.

| Item | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Incident** | An aircraft-operation occurrence that does not qualify as an accident but has affected, or has the potential to affect, operational safety. |
| **Investigation** | An accident-prevention process in which information is obtained and evaluated, conclusions are reached, causes are identified, and safety recommendations are formulated when appropriate. |
| **Serious incident** | An incident arising from circumstances in which an accident came close to occurring. Illustrative cases appear in Section 26.9. |
| **Serious injury** | An injury sustained by a person in an accident and described by the specified criteria: hospitalization exceeding **48 hours**, beginning within **seven days** after the injury; a bone fracture other than a simple fracture of a finger, toe or the nose; a laceration producing severe bleeding or damage to a nerve, muscle or tendon; an injury to an internal organ; second- or third-degree burns or burns covering more than **5%** of the body's surface; or confirmed exposure to infectious substances or harmful radiation. |
| **State of Design** | The State exercising jurisdiction over the organization responsible for the aircraft type design. |
| **State of Manufacture** | The State exercising jurisdiction over the organization responsible for the aircraft's final assembly. |
| **State of Occurrence** | The State within whose territory the accident or incident takes place. |

## Objective of Investigation

### 26.4 Objective

The purpose of investigating accidents and incidents is to prevent further occurrences. Determining who should be blamed, or allocating legal liability, is not an objective of the safety investigation.

## Investigations

### 26.5 Responsibility for Instigating an Investigation

Responsibility for initiating an investigation depends on the circumstances and location of the occurrence.

- **Within a Contracting State:** When an accident or serious incident occurs in the territory of a State that is party to the Convention, the State of Occurrence initiates the investigation.
- **Within a non-Contracting State:** If the occurrence is in a State that is not a Contracting State, the State of Registry should make an effort to initiate an investigation.
- **Outside the territory of any State, or at an unknown location:** The State of Registry is responsible for initiating the investigation.
- **Where the State of Occurrence does not investigate:** The State of Registry or the State of the Operator may conduct an investigation if the State of Occurrence declines to do so.

### 26.6 Participation

The following States possess an entitlement to participate through representatives in an investigation:

- The **State of Registry**.
- The **State of the Operator**.
- The **State of Design**.
- The **State of Manufacture**.

A further entitlement applies to any State that supplies information, facilities or specialist personnel to the investigating State in response to its request.

Where citizens of another State have been killed or seriously injured, that State may request permission to appoint an expert. An expert appointed under this provision is entitled to:

- Attend the accident location.
- Examine the relevant factual information.
- Take part in establishing the identities of the victims.
- Assist with interviews of surviving citizens of the appointing State.
- Obtain a copy of the final investigation report.

### 26.7 Final Report

The State conducting an accident investigation must distribute its final report without unnecessary delay to the appropriate recipients. These comprise:

- The State which initiated the investigation.
- The State of Registry.
- The State of the Operator.
- The State of Design.
- The State of Manufacture.
- States whose nationals sustained fatal or non-fatal injuries.
- States that contributed relevant information, substantial facilities or specialist expertise.

### 26.8 ICAO

ICAO must receive notification of an accident or serious incident involving an aircraft whose maximum mass is **more than 2,250 kg**.

For aircraft with a maximum mass **exceeding 5,700 kg**, a copy of the completed investigation report must also be submitted to ICAO.

## Serious Incidents

### 26.9 Examples

The circumstances below illustrate occurrences that are likely to meet the classification of a serious incident. They provide guidance rather than a comprehensive or definitive list.

- An aircraft comes close to colliding with another aircraft or object, such that an avoiding manoeuvre is required to prevent collision or an unsafe situation, or such a manoeuvre would have been appropriate.
- A collision with terrain during controlled flight is avoided by only a small margin.
- A take-off is rejected on a runway that is closed or already occupied.
- An aircraft takes off from a closed or occupied runway, passing obstacles with only marginal clearance.
- A landing is made, or attempted, on a runway that is closed or occupied.
- Actual performance during take-off or the initial climb falls substantially short of the predicted performance.
- Fire or smoke occurs in the passenger cabin or cargo compartment, or an engine fire develops, even where extinguishing equipment successfully puts the fire out.
- An emergency situation requires flight crew members to use oxygen.
- A structural failure or engine break-up occurs but the event does not meet the accident classification.
- Several malfunctions affecting one or more aircraft systems seriously interfere with operation of the aircraft.
- A flight crew member becomes incapacitated while the aircraft is airborne.
- The remaining fuel quantity is such that the pilot has to declare an emergency.
- A take-off or landing occurrence involves an undershoot, an overrun or a departure through the side of a runway.
- A system failure, weather event, operation outside the approved flight envelope or another occurrence creates the potential for serious difficulty in controlling the aircraft.
- More than one component of a redundant system required for flight guidance and navigation fails.

## EU Considerations

### 26.10 EU Directive 94/56

**Directive 94/56** provides the European Community arrangements concerning aircraft accident investigation, adapting the principles established in ICAO Annex 13 to the European framework.

**Objective**

The Directive seeks to enhance aviation safety by ensuring that investigations are undertaken promptly and focus solely on avoiding future accidents and incidents. Its scope includes civil aviation accidents and incidents occurring in European Community territory and those involving aircraft registered in a Member State.

**Requirements and organization**

Each Member State must arrange for the investigation of every accident and serious incident, with the intention of preventing a recurrence. Investigations into less serious incidents are also encouraged.

The technical safety investigation must remain separate from proceedings about legal responsibility. Investigators are not tasked with allocating blame or determining liability.

A **permanent, independent air accident investigation body** must conduct or supervise the technical investigations in every Member State.

To enable investigators in charge to complete their duties effectively and as quickly as possible, they must be provided unrestricted access to:

- The accident site and the aircraft concerned.
- The available inventory or listing of evidence.
- The aircraft's flight recorders.
- Results from examinations of victims' bodies and from tests on samples obtained from them.

**Reporting and safety recommendations**

An accident investigation must result in a report containing safety recommendations. The investigating body must release the final report publicly as soon as practicable, preferably **within 12 months** of the accident.

Member States must establish measures so that the investigation body's safety recommendations receive proper consideration and are acted upon.

### 26.11 EU Directive 2003/42

**Directive 2003/42** addresses the reporting of occurrences relevant to aviation safety.

**Objective**

The purpose of the occurrence-reporting arrangements is to increase aviation safety by ensuring that relevant safety information is submitted, gathered, retained, safeguarded and shared. The process exists to prevent accidents and incidents, not to establish fault or liability.

**Occurrence**

An occurrence is an interruption of operations, a defect, a malfunction or another abnormal circumstance that has affected, or might have affected, flight safety without resulting in an accident or a serious incident.

**Reporting arrangements**

Under the Directive:

- Member States must require safety occurrences to be reported to their competent authorities.
- Each Member State must appoint at least one competent authority to establish a system for receiving, assessing, processing and retaining occurrence reports.
- Member States must exchange relevant safety information, making it accessible to the competent authorities of the other Member States and to the Commission.
- The Directive specifies detailed examples of occurrences for which reporting is required.

## Questions

**1.** While an aircraft is taxiing towards the departure runway, one wheel becomes embedded in mud and the aircraft is damaged. How should this occurrence be classified?

a. An incident.  
b. An accident.  
c. A serious incident.  
d. An ordinary operational hazard.

**2.** Which State or States may bear responsibility for arranging an accident investigation, depending on the circumstances of the occurrence?

a. The State of Occurrence.  
b. The State of Registry.  
c. ICAO.  
d. Both a and b.

**3.** The purpose of an accident investigation is to prevent future accidents. What additional purpose is included?

a. Determining blame.  
b. Improving manufacturing design as a separate stated objective.  
c. Supporting legal proceedings.  
d. None of these; prevention is its objective.

**4.** An aircraft approaching its parking apron unintentionally leaves the taxiway, enters a grassed area and drops a wheel into a hole. The resulting damage is severe enough to delay the aircraft's next departure. Which statement correctly describes the occurrence and the required response?

a. Because no one is injured and the flight has finished, the only action necessary is to inform the insurer, maintenance personnel, operator and those responsible for the taxiway and runway areas.  
b. This constitutes an accident, and the flight crew must follow the applicable accident procedures.  
c. This is an incident that the captain must report to the aerodrome authority within 48 hours.  
d. This is merely an operational irregularity, for which notification of the repair-related delay to the operator is sufficient.

**5.** Which of the following States has the right to be represented during an aircraft accident investigation?

a. The State of Registry.  
b. The State of Design.  
c. The State of Manufacture.  
d. All three States.
