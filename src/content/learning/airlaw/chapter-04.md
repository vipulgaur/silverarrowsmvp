# Chapter 4: Aircraft Nationality and Registration Marks

## Introduction

### 4.1 International Requirements — Annex 7

Aircraft registration and the display of identifying marks are international obligations that originated in the **Paris Convention of 1919**. Every aircraft is required to be registered and to display two identifying elements: a **nationality mark** and a **registration mark**.

The detailed international provisions are contained in **Annex 7 to the Chicago Convention**, entitled *Aircraft Nationality and Registration Marks*. Unlike Annexes that combine Standards with Recommended Practices, **Annex 7 contains Standards only**.

In exceptional circumstances, the appropriate authority may authorize a temporary exemption. Examples include:

- Permitting an **experimental prototype** to undertake test flights before its normal registration arrangements are completed.
- Allowing an **historic or formerly military aircraft** to operate temporarily without the usual external registration markings.

## Nationality and Registration Marks

### 4.2 Differences Notified to ICAO

Contracting States do not necessarily apply every provision of Annex 7 in exactly the same way. For this reason, the Annex identifies States according to the information they have supplied to ICAO about their national requirements. The listed categories are:

1. **States that have declared differences** between their national rules and the ICAO Standards in Annex 7.
2. **States that have declared no differences** between their national rules and those Standards.
3. **States that have not supplied information** to ICAO on whether differences exist.

These distinctions concern the information reported by the States about compliance with Annex 7.

### 4.3 Composition and Allocation of Aircraft Marks

A complete aircraft identification normally consists of a **nationality mark followed by an individual registration mark**. For example:

**`G-ABCD`**

In this example:

- **`G`** is the nationality mark.
- **`ABCD`** is the registration mark.
- The **hyphen (`-`)** separates the two parts where required by the applicable character arrangement.

The nationality mark **always comes before** the registration mark. A hyphen must be placed before the first character of the registration mark when that character is of the same type as the final character of the nationality mark.

#### Nationality Mark

A State selects its nationality mark from the **nationality symbols contained in the series of radio call signs assigned to it by the International Telecommunication Union (ITU)**, a specialized agency of the United Nations.

The **State of Registry informs ICAO** of the nationality mark it uses. Depending on the allocation, this mark may comprise:

- One letter.
- Several letters.
- Letters combined with numbers.

A distinctive symbol associated with a State may also be incorporated. One example referred to in the rules is **the Red Cross symbol associated with Switzerland**.

#### Registration Mark

The registration mark identifies the individual aircraft within the relevant register. It may use **letters, numbers, or both**.

The **State of Registry**, or where applicable a **common mark registering authority**, assigns the mark from identifiers available for use in its register. A mark already allocated to another aircraft in that register is not selected again as an available mark.

### 4.4 Common Marks

An aircraft operated by an undertaking registered in **more than one country** may use a **common mark** in place of a nationality mark.

For such an arrangement:

- The **ITU allocates the common mark**.
- **ICAO designates a State** to carry out the responsibilities normally belonging to the State of Registry.
- That State acts as the **common mark registering authority**.
- The registering authority also assumes the corresponding functions relating to the aircraft's **continuing airworthiness**.

The example identified here is **`4YB`**, allocated to **Arab Air Cargo Incorporated**, described as operating from **Jordan and Iraq**. Under this arrangement, **Jordan** was designated to discharge the other functions of the common mark registering authority.

### 4.5 Prohibited Letter Combinations

Certain letter sequences cannot be used as an aircraft registration mark, or as part of one, because they have established meanings in international distress, urgency, safety, or signalling communications. Using them as aircraft identifiers could lead to confusion.

The excluded combinations include:

| Prohibited combination | Established use |
|---|---|
| **`SOS`** | Morse distress signal |
| **`PAN`** | Urgency signal |
| **`XXX`** | Morse urgency signal |
| **`TTT`** | Morse safety signal (*sécurité*) |
| **Q-code groups** | Established communications codes, such as `QNH`, `QRT`, and `QUG` |
| **Five-letter groups from the International Code of Signals** | Internationally assigned signalling combinations |

These restrictions apply to the use of the combinations in the **registration portion** of the aircraft's identification.

## Certification of Registration

### 4.6 Certificate of Registration: Purpose and Required Contents

The **Certificate of Registration** is the official confirmation that an aircraft has been entered on the register of its **State of Registry**. It must be **carried on board the aircraft at all times**.

The certificate identifies the aircraft, its ownership, and the act of registration. It includes all of the following particulars:

1. The **nationality mark or common mark**.
2. The aircraft's **registration mark**.
3. The **manufacturer's designation** of the aircraft.
4. The aircraft's **serial number**.
5. The **name and address of the owner**.
6. An official certification that the aircraft **has been entered on the State's aircraft register**.
7. The **signature of the registering officer, together with the date**.

## Aircraft Markings

### 4.7 Permanence and Visibility

Nationality or common marks and registration marks must be **painted onto the aircraft** or applied using another method that provides a comparable degree of permanence.

Throughout use, the markings are required to remain **clean and clearly visible**.

### 4.8 Position on Heavier-than-Air Aircraft

For a heavier-than-air aircraft, the required identifying marks are displayed in specified external positions. The positions described are:

- The **underside of a wing**.
- The **fuselage between the wings and the tail**.
- The **upper half of the vertical tail surface**.

### 4.9 Minimum Character Height

Different minimum character heights apply according to the surface carrying the marking:

| Marking position | Minimum height of characters |
|---|---:|
| **Underside of the wing** (horizontal surface) | **50 cm** |
| **Fuselage or equivalent surface** | **30 cm** |
| **Vertical tail surface** | **30 cm** |
