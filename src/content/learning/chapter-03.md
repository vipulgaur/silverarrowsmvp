## 1. Airflow Around a Three-Dimensional Wing

Airflowing over and under an aerofoil generates an aerodynamic force resolved into Lift (acting perpendicular to the relative airflow) and Drag (acting parallel and opposite to the relative airflow).

![Fig-03-01: Aerofoil Terminology and Pressure Distribution](Cross-section of an aerofoil showing chord line, camber, leading edge, trailing edge, angle of attack, relative airflow, and centre of pressure)

## 2. Primary Effects of the Elevator, Ailerons and Rudder

Each primary flight control produces an immediate movement about its respective axis: elevator pitches the nose up or down, ailerons roll the wings, and rudder yaws the nose left or right.

![Fig-03-02: Pitch, Roll and Yaw Axes of the Aeroplane](Three-axis diagram of an aeroplane illustrating pitch about the lateral axis, roll about the longitudinal axis, and yaw about the normal axis)

## 3. Further Effects of Roll and Yaw (Slipstream, Torque and Asymmetric Blade Effect)

A roll initiated by ailerons leads to a sideslip and a secondary yaw towards the lower wing, while yaw initiated by rudder causes the outer wing to travel faster, producing a secondary roll.

![Fig-03-03: Further Effect of Aileron and Rudder Inputs](Sequence diagram showing initial roll with ailerons followed by sideslip and secondary yaw, and initial yaw with rudder followed by differential wing speed and secondary roll)

## 4. Effects of Slipstream and Airspeed on Control Effectiveness

Propeller slipstream spirals corkscrew-fashion around the fuselage, striking the fin on the port side and increasing elevator and rudder responsiveness even at low forward airspeed.

![Fig-03-04: Helical Propeller Slipstream and Fin Incidence](Top and side view of helical propeller slipstream striking the vertical fin and tailplane during high-power low-airspeed flight)

## 5. Use of Trim and Effects of Ancillary Controls

Correct trimming removes control column forces after power or attitude changes; extending flaps increases both lift and drag while shifting the centre of pressure and pitching moment.
